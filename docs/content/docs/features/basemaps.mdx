## Basemap sources

The **Basemap** section under Sources offers several satellite/hybrid basemaps ([Google Hybrid](https://mapsplatform.google.com/), [Bing Aerial](https://www.bing.com/maps/aerial), [ESRI World Imagery](https://www.esri.com/en-us/arcgis/products/arcgis-image/services/world-imagery), [Mapbox Satellite](https://www.mapbox.com/), [HERE Satellite](https://www.here.com/), [Google Satellite](https://mapsplatform.google.com/), [OpenStreetMap](https://www.openstreetmap.org/)) plus any custom source you've added — see [Bring Your Own Data](/features/byod).

OpenStreetMap is [OpenFreeMap](https://openfreemap.org/)'s Liberty vector style rather than raster tiles, so it stays crisp at any zoom. **OSM 3D buildings**, the checkbox at the end of the list, extrudes OSM buildings with their recorded heights (OpenFreeMap Liberty, from z14) over any basemap, Google or Esri included, or over a terrain-only view; off by default. The cog beside it sets their look: Liberty's grey, white, black or a colour of your own, the opacity, and footprint outlines (a line under each building's base; MapLibre's extrusions have no edges of their own).

## Overlays

Overlays have an opacity pill each, and the **Overlays** heading one for all of them (`overlaysOpacity`): click a pill and a short vertical slider opens on it, its point for the current value under the pointer, so a drag up or down sets it (bottom 0, top 100).

Overlays are listed and drawn in both basemap modes, **Simple** and **Split** (before 2026-11-24 a checked overlay drew nothing in Simple mode). A hidden basemap or overlay (unchecked, the Basemap section off, opacity 0, or a view of a split that does not draw the basemap) loads no tiles, and unchecking an overlay no longer switches the Basemap section back on.

### Transparency

A tiled or WMS source's edit dialog (the pencil on its row, in edit mode) has a **Transparency** field under Opacity, for what the tiles' own alpha becomes (until 2026-11-24 it was a button on the overlay row):

| Mode | What it draws | For |
|---|---|---|
| **As served** | The tiles as the service sends them | Most overlays |
| **Opaque** | Any partial alpha raised to full; no-data stays transparent | ArcGIS Online's 8-bit layers, which bake an alpha of about one half into every pixel |
| **Dark over (multiply)** | Black, the dark pixels opaque and the light ones transparent: exactly a multiply blend, the imagery shaded by the render | A sky view factor, a hillshade, a slope over a photo |
| **Light over (screen)** | White, the light pixels opaque and the dark ones transparent: a screen blend | A local dominance (bright mounds on a dark ground) |

The choice is kept with the overlay, like its opacity. Until you pick one, the Library entry's default applies (marked in the list): Opaque for the Maya sky view factors, Light over for the Maya local dominance layers (their ground is dark and the mounds bright, so Opaque drew a black sheet with white specks), Opaque for Berlin's and IGN's hillshades.

An overlay draws over the basemap instead of replacing it. Where the overlays sit in the layer stack is one setting for all of them, **Stack** in the Overlays group (`overlaysStack` in the link): **Under relief** (the default: hillshade and the colour relief draw over them), **Over hypso** (above the colour relief, still shaded by the hillshade) or **On top** (above every terrain layer, only contours and markers over them). Their order among themselves is the list's order (drag the handles in edit mode). The Basemap and Overlays groups fold. In a split or grid with per-view basemaps, each overlay row gets the view grid, so an overlay can be on view B only; clicking the overlay's name adds it to every view, or removes it from all of them when every view already shows it.

## Pre-rendered relief

The basemap Library has a section of its own, **Pre-rendered relief (archaeology)**, folded at first: shaded and enhanced relief that a publisher has already rendered from lidar or a national elevation model, served as imagery. Each one stacks as an overlay over any terrain; the app's own visualization modes do not apply to it, since there is no elevation behind it. A search for "archaeology", "relief" or "sky view factor" finds the whole section, and it opens on its own while a search is typed.

| Area | What is in it |
|---|---|
| Maya lowlands | Tikal, Holmul, La Corona, Chactun (hillshade, sky view factor, local dominance), the Alianza M-REDD+ blocks and transects and NASA G-LiHT strips (blended renders), by Tulane's MARI-GISLAB, **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, non-commercial**; El Pilar (UCSB bare-earth hillshade); the Holmul 1 m DEM as a grey picture (Tulane, cut in EPSG:4326 and resampled in the browser, licence not stated) |
| Americas | USGS 3DEP (multi-directional hillshade, slope, tinted hillshade, the shaded relief basemap), Canada HRDEM lidar (hillshade, slope), Mexico INEGI hillshade, Kentucky lidar hillshades, Pennsylvania lidar hillshades (USGS 2017-2020 and PAMAP 2006-2008) |
| British Isles and the North | England EA 1 m DTM hillshade, Wales 1 m DTM tinted hillshade and DSM hillshade, Ireland GSI lidar hillshades (heritage surveys, OPW, TII), Norway, Sweden (hillshade, slope), Finland, Iceland (IslandsDEM hillshade and **topographic position index**) |
| Benelux | AHN4 multi-directional hillshade, Flanders 25 cm hillshade and **sky view factor**, Wallonia hillshade and slope, Luxembourg lidar 2019 |
| Central Europe | basemap.de, Bavaria, NRW, Saxony, Brandenburg, Berlin, Mecklenburg-Vorpommern, Rhineland-Palatinate, Thuringia; basemap.at terrain and surface, Styria, Upper Austria; swissALTI3D and swissSURFACE3D; Czech DMR 5G and DMP 1G; Slovakia; Poland |
| Southern Europe | IGN LiDAR HD and RGE ALTI hillshades, slope and hypsometric tints; IGN Spain, Catalonia, Navarra, Basque Country; Portugal's coastal lidar; South Tyrol; Sardinia (1 m lidar and 10 m hillshades, licence not stated) |
| Asia-Pacific | GSI hillshade, **red relief map** (赤色立体地図), hypsometric tints and slope; CS立体図 for Nagano and Hyogo; LINZ hillshades; Tasmania, Queensland |

Which research and national portals were checked for more, and why some are not here (a key, a national-grid-only cache, no CORS, download-only data), is on [Research lidar endpoints](/dev/research-lidar-endpoints).

Each entry carries the publisher's credit and licence (the info link opens its page). Most are open (CC BY, CC0, OGL, Etalab, Datenlizenz Deutschland); the Maya renders are not for commercial use, and a few endpoints are viewer back ends with no published terms for outside use (their descriptions say so).

Two source settings fix what some of these services publish. ArcGIS Online serves an 8-bit layer (a sky view factor, a local dominance) with an alpha of about one half baked into every pixel, so at 100 % it still showed half the map through: the `alphaMode` "opaque" (formerly `forceOpaque`) draws every pixel with data fully opaque, and the overlay's [Transparency](#transparency) menu offers the other modes. A service with no overview tiles (the Maya renders start at zoom 11, 12 or 15) vanished when the view zoomed out: `underzoom` assembles the tiles of up to three zoom levels below its first one from the tiles at that level, fetching only those inside the source's extent. See the [source schema](/dev/source-schema#the-record).

## A basemap per view

In **split view** (grid layouts up to 4×2 / 8 views, labeled A–H), each basemap row shows an A–H button grid so a different source can be assigned per view. Clicking a row's **label** (not just its A–H buttons) sets *every* active view in the current grid layout to that source in one click — handy for putting a whole 4×2 comparison grid onto Historical Imagery without clicking each pane individually.

![Comparing basemap sources side-by-side in grid mode](/screenshots/n-grid.jpg)

<p className="text-sm text-fd-muted-foreground italic">Basemap comparison, Side grid mode</p>

A georeferenced IIIF map (an Allmaps overlay) is drawn one of two ways, chosen in its dialog under **Draw it**: **warped in the browser**, the sharpest, from the IIIF image itself, or from **Allmaps' tile server** (`allmaps.xyz`), warped and cached server-side, a plain raster that tilts and drapes on terrain and is much faster when the image server is slow (David Rumsey's). The in-browser warp draws in flat views only (Allmaps' layer has no pitch support yet, see [Upstream requests](/dev/upstream-requests)), so a tilted view switches it to the tile server by itself and back at pitch 0; a toast says so the first time. Auto picks the tile server for a David Rumsey link.

Why the in-browser warp is sharper: it samples the original IIIF image at the exact scale of the screen and warps each pixel on the GPU, one resampling. The tile server renders 256 px PNG tiles at fixed integer zooms, and the map resamples those again when it draws them: two resamplings plus PNG quantisation. The tile server is slow on a first visit to an area because it has to fetch the source tiles from the image server and warp them; after that the tiles are cached and come back in a fraction of a second.

## Remove paper

![Map Warper's Paris 1905 as an overlay over ESRI World Imagery, Remove paper off: the cream paper of the sheet hides the imagery](/screenshots/remove-paper-off.jpg)

![The same map with Remove paper on: the paper is transparent, the streets show the imagery under the drawn buildings, the Seine and the parks keep their colour; the sidebar lists the detected paper colour, threshold and paper share](/screenshots/remove-paper-on.jpg)

<p className="text-sm text-fd-muted-foreground italic">Remove paper on (Auto): the paper detected as #ebd2b3, threshold 0.20, 68% of the sheet; the buildings, the river and the parks stay</p>

**Remove paper** turns the paper of scanned maps transparent, so an old map reads over today's imagery. It is in the **Basemap** section, under the **Overlays** heading (Bring Your Own Data), and only shows while a scanned map is on a view: a warped Allmaps map, or a tiled old map from Map Warper, Wikimaps Warper, SLUB, the USGS quads, or the swisstopo, Kartverket, NGI and Academia Sinica map series (as an overlay, or as a view's basemap picked on the timeline). It works like the Allmaps viewer's magic wand: the pixels close to the paper colour fade out.

- **Auto** (the default): each map gets its own paper colour and threshold from its own image. For an Allmaps map that is a 256 px rendition inside its georeference mask; for a tiled map, one tile at the centre of its extent. The luminance histogram is split in two by Otsu's method, the paper colour is the median of the bright part, and the threshold is where the pixels' distance to that colour falls away (at most 0.2, so an aged sheet keeps its colour washes).
- The line under the switch shows, per map, the paper colour, the threshold in use and the paper's share of the sheet, or "no paper, kept".
- A map is left as it is when its drawing fills the sheet (an engraving, a grey bird's-eye view): the paper must be light and cover 40% of the sheet, or three quarters when it is darker.
- The slider widens or narrows every detected threshold; the middle is as detected.
- **Auto** off: one colour (the swatch) and one threshold (the slider) for every map.

Limits: the removal is by colour, so map details close to the paper colour (a light road fill, pale washes) fade with it, and white margins outside the drawn frame go only if they are close to the paper colour. The setting is kept in your browser, not in the link.
