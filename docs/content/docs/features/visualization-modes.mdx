## Elevation Hypso (Hypsometric Tint)

Color-encoded elevation with a choice of color ramps (including [CPT City](http://seaviewsensing.com/pub/cpt-city/) ramps, and in the Classic tab **Relief Green-Red**, the green-to-red palette of archaeological LiDAR figures: pale cyan for water and the lowest ground, greens, yellow and orange up to a dark red top) — see [MapLibre PR #5913](https://github.com/maplibre/maplibre-gl-js/pull/5913). The ramps are grouped in tabs (Top Qgs, Classic, Topo, TopoBath, Temp, CET, SDR), and some ramps are listed in two of them. A link carries the tab with the ramp, `colorRamp=<name>&colorRampTab=<tab>`, so it reopens on the tab it was picked from; a link without `colorRampTab` opens on the first tab that holds the ramp.

## Contours + Geo Grid

Contour lines trace one of two references. **Absolute** is real elevation, with a minor and a major interval. Slide Minor to its first stop, Off, to draw major lines only. **LRM** traces the Local Relief Model instead, so the lines follow local bumps and hollows rather than altitude.

Configurable-interval contour lines via [onthegomap/maplibre-contour](https://github.com/onthegomap/maplibre-contour), plus a lat/lng graticule overlay.

### Iso-line

![An iso-slope at 45° with its fill over the Matterhorn hillshade: the red line where the slope crosses 45°, the area steeper than 45° filled in light red, with the Iso-line controls open](/screenshots/viz-modes/isoline-slope.jpg)

<p className="text-sm text-fd-muted-foreground italic">Iso-line, Slope at 45° with Fill the area above, over Hillshade, with the Contours & GeoGrid panel open, Matterhorn massif · <a href="https://terrain-viewer.iconem.com/?lat=45.9763&lng=7.6586&zoom=12.6&pitch=0&bearing=0&viewMode=3d&showHillshade=true&hillshadeOpacity=1&exaggeration=1&showContoursAndGraticules=true&showContours=false&showIsoline=true&isolineMeasure=slope&isolineMode=value&isolineValue=45&isolineFill=true&isolineFillOpacity=0.35" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">open in app ↗</a></p>

**Iso-line** is the third group of **Contours & GeoGrid**, under the geogrid (**Show Iso-line**): a vector line drawn from any **measure**, independent of the contour lines. The measure is the elevation, any terrain-analysis mode (slope, aspect, TRI, TPI, roughness, curvature, shape index, blobness, eigen ratio, orientation), the local relief model, or a lighting mode's brightness (Phong with its rim, Matcap, the hard shadow), each with that mode's own settings (the curvature kernel, the LRM radius, the light, the Phong strengths and Fresnel rim). The horizon modes (sky-view factor, openness, local dominance) are left out: minutes per view at the zooms a line needs. **At a value** draws one line where the measure crosses the value you set (an iso-slope at 30° for avalanche terrain or 80° for cliffs, a lake or flood level, canopy on an nDSM at 1.5 m), the slider sized to the measure's range and unit, the exact value typed; **Fill the area above** paints that area in the line's color. Both come from one set of polygons (marching squares on the measure tiles, `isoband://`), so the fill stops exactly on the line. **Smoothing** (0 to 16 px, `isolineSmooth`) blurs the measure before they are traced, for a line that follows the shape of an area rather than every pixel. **Every interval** draws the contours of the measure itself (slope every 10°, a curvature every 1). The lines export as GeoJSON like the contours (`showIsoline`, `isolineMeasure`, `isolineMode`, `isolineValue`, `isolineInterval`, `isolineFill`). The tiles follow the terrain source's own zoom (a WMS DSM up to zoom 19), so the line is as sharp as the shading.

Under the hood every derived mode's tiles are a Terrarium-encoded scalar, so the contour engine reads them like a DEM; a lighting tile goes through `luma://`, which composites it over a mid grey and keeps its luminance (128 neutral, 0 full shadow, 255 the brightest highlight; with the diffuse strength at 0 and a dark rim the whole range sits below 45, so pick the value accordingly); the value you type is scaled the way the mode bakes its numbers (curvature ×100) before the polygons are traced.

Iso-line replaces the beta **threshold outlines** of the contours (a third contour reference, now removed, with its `thresholdBeta` flag): an outline at 1.5 m on an nDSM, or a flood level on a DEM, is now an Iso-line on the elevation at that value. Limits: the line is traced on the measure's tiles, so it is only as fine as the terrain source.


**Cliff teeth** (slope, at a value): teeth along the line, the symbol maps use for cliffs. They sit on a simplified outline of the steep areas, traced on the slope blurred over 4 px with specks under 40 px dropped, so they follow the faces rather than every pixel; the thin full-resolution line stays underneath. The outline is the boundary of the area steeper than the value, so both its edges carry teeth, hanging off the steep side; OSM-style cliff lines (a mapped edge that stops where the cliff stops) need vector data the DEM does not carry.

- **Slope** — magnitude of the gradient
- **Aspect** — direction of the gradient
- **Curvature** — rate of slope change: Profile (flow acceleration), Plan/Divergence (flow convergence), Det Hessian (blob/saddle detector), or Combined (discrete Laplacian)
- **TRI** (Terrain Ruggedness Index) — mean elevation difference to neighbors
- **TPI** (Topographic Position Index) — elevation relative to the neighborhood mean
- **Roughness** — max − min elevation in a neighborhood
- **Blobness** — structure-tensor measure of gradient-direction variance; high at peaks/pits/saddles/knolls
- **Shape Index**, **Eigen Ratio**, **Orientation** — further curvature-tensor-derived shape descriptors

## Relief Visualization

SVF/Openness/Local Dominance share the terrain-analysis pipeline above (just a ray-marched kernel instead of a 3×3 one); LRM's pyramid-ancestor mechanism is different enough to get [its own page](/dev/lrm).

- **LRM (Local Relief Model)** — raw elevation minus a low-pass-filtered version, isolating small features from large-scale topography (the low-pass layer comes from a coarser pyramid tile, bilinearly upsampled)
- **nDSM / Difference** — this view's terrain minus another terrain source (the subtrahend): building and tree heights for a DSM minus a DTM, or change between two dates. The view keeps its own terrain for hillshade, contours and 3D, and the mode draws the difference over it, with its own ramp and range (Heights: 0 to 30 m, the ground left transparent; Change: diverging, ±10 m), an Invert switch and a vertical offset that can be measured from the screen. On one view of a split, like any mode. See [nDSM and DEM Difference](/features/ndsm-and-comparison#two-ways-a-source-or-a-mode)
- **Sky-View Factor (SVF)** — fraction of the visible sky hemisphere from each point (low in enclosed pits/canyons, high on open summits/ridges), from a ray-marched horizon angle in 8 directions ([Zakšek, Oštir & Kokalj, 2011](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/3/2/398))
- **Openness** — mean angular distance from zenith to the horizon across the same 8 directions; **Positive** highlights ridges/summits, **Negative** highlights valleys/pits
- **Local Dominance** — local visibility-weighted elevation contrast

Other tools that compute these modes (gdaldem, RVT, MapLibre's own hillshade) and the papers behind each, with how the app's kernels differ from them: [Terrain Analysis Pipeline, related work](/dev/terrain-analysis-pipeline#related-work-and-references); for the local relief model (Hesse's full method, the multi-scale relief model): [LRM, related work](/dev/lrm#related-work-and-references).

## Lighting Effects

See [Lighting Effects](/dev/lighting-effects) for how these differ from the terrain-analysis pipeline above (real surface normals, GPU shading, a live-WebGL fast path), and [Sun Position](/features/sun-position) for the astronomy behind driving Phong's light from real solar position.

- **Matcap** — material-capture-style shading looked up by surface normal, independent of any real light direction; the **Duotone Sphere** material matches the Duotone NW/NE hillshade
- **Phong** — ambient + diffuse + specular shading from a compass-fixed (or camera-relative) light, sharing the "hold L, drag" on-map light control. With the live renderer it takes up to three colored lights: in Free mode, Light Direction shows **Lights 1 / 2 / 3** and a color swatch per light, and lights 2 and 3 are pills of their color on the light pad (blue from the north-east and orange from the south-east by default). **Fresnel Rim** (under Intensities, with **Rim Falloff**) brightens the edges of slopes that turn away from the camera; it shows best on a tilted view. Details in [Lighting Effects](/dev/lighting-effects#up-to-three-colored-lights-live-renderer).
- **Shadows**, two kinds sharing one light (the same pad, or the datetime sun):
  - **Terrain (+ buildings if DSM)** (hard shadows) — cast shadows of the elevation source toward the sun's actual azimuth/altitude: the bare ground with a terrain model (DTM, such as Mapterhorn), buildings and trees too with a surface model (DSM). On real relief most slopes are under 30°, so long cast shadows need a low sun (the outer rim of the light pad: the pad maps its radius to cos(altitude), so 30° and below is the outer 13 %). **Renderer → Live (GPU)** (default, `shadowRenderer=live`, `lib/shadow-live-gl-layer.ts`): every pixel marches its own ray toward the sun on the GPU, over a Float32 mosaic of the view's DEM tiles and then over a mosaic three zooms coarser reaching tens of kilometres beyond the view, with the Earth's curvature (and refraction) lowering distant ground; the sun slider, the Datetime hours and the Sun Shadow Calculator move the shadows in real time, no Search Radius and no height tolerance. **Edge → Soft** (default, `shadowSoft`) draws the penumbra of the sun's 0.53° disc, wider the farther the ridge casting it; **Hard** cuts at the sun's centre. **Renderer → Tiles (CPU)** (`shadowRenderer=raster`) keeps the `shadow://` tiles, computed per tile on the CPU within the Search Radius, hard-edged, flat Earth; exports and the Iso-line always use them (an export reads tiles at any size and extent, the Iso-line needs a raster of values), and they take over when the GPU layer cannot run on the device. Both work on mercator, globe and 3D terrain (draped by MapLibre) and in every split view. Limits of the live renderer: the view mosaic holds at most 48 DEM tiles, so a wide or strongly pitched view uses a coarser zoom for the shadows; shadows cast from beyond the far mosaic are missed; the elevation is not exaggerated (as the tiles). How the two compare: [Shadow Renderers](/dev/shadow-renderers).
  - **Buildings (Vector shadows)** — shadows of buildings, from the footprints and heights of a vector source (**Buildings from**: OpenStreetMap through OpenFreeMap's building layer by default, OSM height or levels; Overture Maps; or a vector source of your own with a height field, `buildingShadowSource`), the fast flat-ground way, on the GPU: each footprint is swept away from the sun by height / tan(altitude), the sweeps are drawn once into a mask (overlaps never darken twice, roofs stay lit), and the mask is composited over the map. The buildings are read once per settled view, from zoom 13; moving the light is a repaint, so the sun slider is live. Needs no OSM basemap and no vector overlay on. Mercator only, and over 3D terrain the shadows stay on the flat plane the buildings' feet are drawn on; for shadows cast by the real shapes (a spire, a dome) falling on slopes, use the terrain Shadows above with a surface model (DSM) as the terrain source. Off by default (`showBuildingShadows=true`, `buildingShadowOpacity`). With the Datetime light they fall where the sun puts them at a given date and hour: see [Sun Position, Building shadows](/features/sun-position#building-shadows) for morning, noon and afternoon views of Manhattan.

### Terrain shadows: reach, Earth curvature, soft edge

The live renderer follows each shadow ray past the edge of the view. **Beyond the view, the ray continues over the same terrain three zoom levels coarser**, about 81 × 81 km around the view at zoom 13 in the Alps and hundreds of kilometres at zoom 11. So a ridge off screen still shades the valley you are looking at, and a low sun casts the full shadow of a range across a plain. Before this, the march stopped a tile or two past the view and those shadows were missing.

- **Accuracy.** Agreement with a long-reach reference (the same march with five times denser steps, reaching up to 170 km toward the sun) rose from **0.86–0.98 to 0.98–0.999** (IoU, four cases from Chamonix at a 15° sun to the Po plain at a 1.5° sunset). The cost is **0.2 to 1.5 ms more per frame** for a 1280 × 800 view on a laptop GPU (0.7 to 4.5 ms on the benchmark's 3.1 Mpx mosaic). It is highest where long rays run on past the view, and zero where rays stop early.
- **The Earth's curvature is included.** Distant ground sits below the horizontal by d² / 2R, less about 13 % for atmospheric refraction: 7 m at 10 km, 170 m at 50 km, 690 m at 100 km. The march lowers every sample by that much. Example: under a 1.5° setting sun, the Alps' shadow across the Po plain east of Turin is **87 km long instead of the 107 km a flat Earth gives**, and agreement with the reference goes from 0.91 (flat) to 0.98.
- **Soft or Hard edge** (**Edge**, `shadowSoft`). The sun is a disc 0.53° across, not a point, so a shadow's edge is a penumbra. Its width is the distance to the ridge that casts it × 0.0093, **about 9 m per kilometre of shadow**. Near the ridge the edge is sharp, and it widens toward the shadow's tip. **Soft** (default) draws that penumbra, and **Hard** cuts at the sun's centre. Switching between them redraws at once: it changes one shader value, and nothing is reloaded. The difference only shows where the penumbra spans a few screen pixels, which is why it is hard to see at most zooms:

  | Map zoom (46° N) | One screen pixel | Shadow length for a 3 px penumbra | Penumbra of a 1 km shadow |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 11 | 27 m | 8.6 km | 0.3 px |
  | 13 | 6.6 m | 2.1 km | 1.4 px |
  | 15 | 1.7 m | 540 m | 5.6 px |
  | 17 | 0.4 m | 130 m | 22 px |

  So the soft edge shows on long shadows (a low sun, deep valleys, a range seen from a plain) or when zoomed in close. With a higher sun, most terrain shadows are a few hundred metres long and their penumbra is under a pixel. On the benchmark, 0.8 to 1.4 % of the pixels sit in a penumbra in the Alps at zoom 13, against 22 % for the Alps' 60–100 km shadow over the Po plain. The soft edge costs almost nothing: under 0.1 ms per frame in the mountains, and about 0.4 ms (10 %) on the Po plain, where much of the view is penumbra.

## Tells (Mound Candidate) Detection — Beta

Computes a Difference-of-Gaussians of the LRM as the primary bump signal, keeps only its local maxima (non-maximum suppression scaled to the configured tell size), then vetoes candidates failing any of three shape filters (Blobness, Plan Curvature, Det-Hessian). Opt in via Settings or `?tellsBeta=true`.

<Callout title="Known limitations" type="warn">
COG-streamed sources fetch ancestor (low-pass) tiles with nearest-neighbor resampling only, which can introduce aliasing into the low-pass signal. Detection quality is also capped by the underlying DEM's real resolution — global ~30m tilesets (Copernicus GLO-30-derived) will miss or false-positive on the smaller end of typical Bronze/Iron-Age mound sizes (50–300m across, 3–20m tall). A finer-resolution source (LiDAR, a local high-res COG) improves results where available.
</Callout>
