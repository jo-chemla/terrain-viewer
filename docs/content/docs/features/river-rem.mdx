A **Relative Elevation Model (REM)** shows height above the river instead of height above the sea: the DEM minus a trend surface that follows the river's water level. The slope of the valley is gone, the floodplain reads flat, and what stands a metre or two above or below it shows at once: terraces, levees, old channels, meander scars, point-bar scrolls. The technique was popularized by Dan Coe's maps and automated by the [OpenTopography RiverREM](https://github.com/OpenTopography/RiverREM) Python package.

The **River REM mode** computes one live, in the browser, from whatever terrain source is selected. There is no server: pan to a river and the picture follows.

## Two places to try

**The Allier upstream of Moulins (France).** One of the last freely wandering rivers of western Europe: the active channel belt in pale green, the abandoned channels of the last centuries as a braid of blue threads across the floodplain, the valley sides dark.

## How to use it

Click the sidebar's title and choose **River REM**, or open a link with `appMode=rem`. The sidebar keeps General Settings, the terrain source and the tools, and gets a **River REM** group of three panels in place of the Options group.

### River

Where the centerline comes from:

| Choice | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Longest named river in view** (default) | RiverREM's own rule: of the rivers OpenStreetMap has in view, the one with the most length there. |
| **All waterways in view** | Every waterway found feeds the trend. For a confluence, a delta or a braided reach. |
| **One river, picked by name** | A list of the named rivers found, with their length in view. |
| **Drawn line** | A line drawn with the app's [Drawing tool](/features/tools) (the **Draw** button starts one), or any line already drawn. For paleochannels and historical courses OpenStreetMap does not have, or where its centerline is too coarse. The line can be drawn up or down the river. |

**Rivers / Rivers, streams, canals** widens the OpenStreetMap search from `waterway=river` to streams and canals. **Sample spacing** is the distance between two heights read along the line; 0 spreads 1000 points over the river, as RiverREM does. The line in use is drawn in orange over the REM (**Show the centerline**).

The rivers come from OpenStreetMap through the [Overpass API](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Overpass_API), asked once the view has rested and again only when it leaves the box already searched. Overpass is a shared, volunteer-run service: when a server is slow, another is asked as well; when all refuse, the panel says so and offers **Retry**, and the last rivers found stay in use. Rivers © [OpenStreetMap](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright) contributors.

### Detrending

- **IDW power**: the trend at a place is the average of nearby river heights, weighted by 1 / distance^power. 1 is RiverREM's. Higher values follow the nearest reach more closely.
- **Neighbors**: how many of the nearest river heights are averaged. 0 guesses it from the river's sinuosity, as RiverREM does (2 % of the samples for a straight river, up to five times more for a winding one, between 5 and 100): on a tight meander, too few neighbors print each bend as a step.
- **Downstream cleaning**: a river does not flow uphill, but what a DEM reads along a centerline sometimes does (bridges, banks where the line leaves the water, trees over a narrow stream). **Running minimum** keeps the lowest height met so far going downstream, since all those errors read too high. **Monotone fit** takes the falling profile closest to the heights read, steadier where the DEM has pits. **Off** uses the heights as read, as RiverREM does. The panel reports the water level at both ends and how many heights the cleaning moved.

### Display

A color ramp over a range of heights above the river (0 to 10 m by default), with a **stretch**: **Log** is RiverREM's, most of the ramp spent in the first metres above the water; **Power** with its gamma; **Linear**. The default ramp, *REM Mako*, is RiverREM's look (seaborn's mako reversed: the river pale, high ground dark); *REM Blues* and every other ramp of the app are in the list, with **Invert ramp**. **Opacity** lets a basemap show through, and **Hillshade on top** shades the result with the app's hillshade.

Every setting is in the link (`remRiver`, `remPower`, `remMax`, ... see the [URL reference](/dev/url-api)), the drawn line included.

**Download → Layers** exports the REM as a float32 GeoTIFF in metres above the river, or as colored on screen.

## What is computed

1. **Centerline.** OpenStreetMap ways of one name are joined end to end into lines and cut at the searched box; or the drawn line is taken as is.
2. **Water level.** The DEM is read every *spacing* along the line, from the terrain source's own tiles (any source the other modes can read: XYZ tiles, COGs, VRTs, WMS). A long river is read one or two zoom levels down so that it stays under 48 tiles. The profile is cleaned so that it falls downstream.
3. **Trend surface.** Inverse-distance weighting of the *k* nearest samples, on a grid of 256 cells on its longer side covering the river and the view, read bilinearly.
4. **REM = elevation − trend**, per tile, as a custom MapLibre protocol (`riverrem://`, `lib/river-rem-protocol.ts`) drawn by a `color-relief` layer. A new centerline or parameter changes a revision number in the tile address: the tiles are recomputed, the map is not rebuilt.

### Approximations against RiverREM

- The trend is interpolated on a coarse grid (a cell is 100 to 300 m for a 30 to 80 km view), not at every DEM pixel. The water level varies slowly, so the difference is small away from the channel; inside a narrow channel the grid can smooth a step such as a weir.
- Distances are Web Mercator metres, not a local UTM projection. The weights only depend on ratios of distances, so the effect is negligible at the scale of a view.
- The heights along the river are read at the points of the resampled line (bilinear), where RiverREM burns the line into the DEM grid and takes every pixel it crosses.
- The k nearest neighbors are exact (RiverREM allows 10 % slack for speed).
- The downstream cleaning is an addition: RiverREM uses the heights as read. Set it to **Off** to compare.
- A DEM whose water surface was flattened survey by survey (much of the USGS 3DEP lidar) shows steps in the river itself: they are in the data.

### Split views

The mode draws on every view of a split, but the first version computes everything from **view A's terrain source**: the centerline heights, the trend, and the elevation it is subtracted from. A trend built on one DEM and subtracted from another would mostly show the two surveys' differences.

[River-REM](https://rem.prod.heritagewatch.ai/) ([RiverREM_UI](https://github.com/Iconem/RiverREM_UI)) is Iconem's separate app around the same idea, and the reference this mode was ported from. Use it when you need what this mode leaves out:

- a **server engine**: FastAPI and GDAL running the OpenTopography RiverREM package itself on a UTM mosaic, with the result kept as a full-resolution float32 Cloud-Optimized GeoTIFF and a list of past runs;
- other **interpolations** of the water level (nearest point on the polyline, Euclidean distance transform) next to IDW;
- other **centerline sources** (QLever, OpenStreetMap vector tiles, a GeoJSON or shapefile import).

**Open In → Iconem River-REM** in the sidebar hands the current view over (`https://rem.prod.heritagewatch.ai/?lng=…&lat=…&zoom=…`).

**LRM is the generic cousin.** The [Local Relief Model](/dev/lrm) mode also subtracts a trend from the DEM, but its trend is a blur with no notion of a river: it shows "higher or lower than the neighborhood", where a REM shows "metres above the water".
