The same few terrain modes serve very different sciences: a sky-view factor that finds a Maya causeway also finds a shell crater at Verdun or a sinkhole in Kentucky. This page gathers studies from those fields first, then the methods and elevation models behind the app's modes. Where a study has a clear area, a link opens the app there with the modes it used, on the same data when the app has it, so the method can be seen on real terrain in the browser. Some areas are regional, where a paper covers a whole region or its sites could not be located exactly.

Every reference below was checked against its DOI or publisher page. To suggest one, open an issue on [GitHub](https://github.com/Iconem/terrain-viewer/issues).

## Case studies across fields

Volcanoes, glaciers, fault scarps, battlefields, charcoal kilns and dunes, each read from a DEM or LiDAR with the kind of modes the app offers. Studies that measure change between two surveys (lava volumes, glacier loss, earthquake uplift) correspond to the app's [DEM difference](/features/ndsm-and-comparison).

## Global DEMs

The elevation models a study reaches for when there is no LiDAR, and their accuracy. The app's global terrain is [Mapterhorn](https://mapterhorn.com), which merges them with national data; see [Terrain sources](/features/terrain-sources). Mapterhorn is recent and no published study citing it turned up yet; one comparing GLO-30 with other global models for morphology is under Case studies.

## Using this with an assistant

Each "Open in Terrain Viewer" link is an ordinary URL. An assistant can build the same for any place with the [Terrain Viewer skill or llms.txt](/features/ai-assistants): "show me the sky-view factor over the area of this paper".
