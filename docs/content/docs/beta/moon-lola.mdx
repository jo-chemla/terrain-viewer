Beta. NASA LRO **LOLA** (Lunar Orbiter Laser Altimeter) heights, and the Kaguya Terrain Camera models tied to them, shown as terrain in the viewer, with every relief mode (hillshade, slope, sky-view factor, the [sun position](/features/sun-position) over permanently shadowed craters) computed in the browser as on Earth.

The first exploration (August 2026) planned a re-hosted set of COGs, a CI workflow to build them, a FlatGeobuf tile index and a dedicated `stacdem://` protocol. Main has since gained the pieces that make most of that unnecessary: the [VRT reader](/dev/vrt-protocol) reprojects any VRT in the browser with proj4, honours GDAL's `a_srs=` override, and reads [dataset indexes](/dev/dataset-indexes) kept on a release. A lunar DEM is now a VRT and a URL.

## Putting the Moon on Web Mercator

MapLibre draws the Earth: Web Mercator on the WGS84 sphere, cut at 85.05°. Lunar products are in lunar coordinates (IAU_2015:30100, the 1737.4 km sphere, and its equirectangular and polar stereographic projections, IAU:30115 and friends). No warp is needed for either of the two mappings used here; each is a choice of what the VRT reader is told the coordinates are.

**True position.** Lunar longitude and latitude are drawn as Earth longitude and latitude. It needs nothing at all: proj4js applies no datum shift between a `+R=1737400` sphere and WGS84 when neither carries `towgs84`, so a VRT in its own lunar CRS (a strip's equirectangular metres, or GCS_Moon degrees) comes out at the right lunar lon/lat on the map. Checked: a Kaguya strip whose STAC bbox starts at −26.342° lands at x = 4 659 372 m in IAU:30115, which is what proj4js returns. The OPM Moon basemap uses the same convention, so it lines up. Every horizontal distance is stretched by R⊕/R☾ = 6378.137 / 1737.4 = **3.67**, at every latitude (a lunar degree becomes an Earth degree in both directions), so terrain exaggeration 3.67 restores true proportions. The polar caps past 85.05° fall off the map: Malapert at 86°S is gone.

**Metric fake-geo.** The polar products are south polar stereographic in metres. `?a_srs=EPSG:3857` on the VRT URL relabels those metres as Web Mercator metres: the pole lands at 0,0, lunar 0°E points up (towards the Earth), and because Mercator is true scale at the equator, distances are right and exaggeration 1 is honest (k grows to 1.07 at 60°S, under 0.1 % across the 87°S cap). It is the same trick the Dura Europos project plays with its "fakegeo" COGs. Nothing else on the map means anything there: no basemap, geocoder or place name applies, which is why the project hides them.

The third mapping of the first exploration, a rotated pole (`ob_tran`) drawing one lunar degree as one Earth degree around the pole, needs a real warp and was dropped: the fake-geo mapping covers the poles at true scale without one.

**Heights** stay in metres above the 1737.4 km reference sphere, as every LOLA product gives them, so the elevation picker, contours and exports read lunar heights directly. Two limits from the Earth readers: the VRT reader treats anything above +9 000 m or below −12 000 m as nodata, and the Moon reaches +10 786 m (the Selenean summit near Engel'gardt crater, on the farside), so the very highest summits would draw as holes; and Terrain-RGB bottoms out at −10 000 m, below the Moon's lowest point (−9 130 m, in Antoniadi), so that one is safe. MapLibre's sky, fog and globe curvature are the Earth's.

## Which hosts a browser can read

Checked with real requests on 2026-11-14 (an `Origin` header and a `Range` read):

| Host | Products | CORS | Range | Browser path |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `asc-pds-services.s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com` (USGS Astrogeology mosaics) | LOLA 118 m global, SLDEM2015 59 m (`LolaKaguya_Topo/`), WAC GLD100 100 m, LOLA 87°S 5 m COG, Haworth 1 m SfS | `*` | yes | Only the 87°S COG. The global files are strip TIFFs (one row per strip, no overviews): a tile at full resolution reads about 47 MB of rows, and below that the reader refuses |
| `planetarymaps.usgs.gov/mosaic/…` | The same files | no (the 302 to the S3 bucket carries no CORS header) | yes | Use the S3 URL directly |
| `astrogeo-ard.s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com` | Kaguya TC DTMs v2 (COGs, overviews), LOLA RDR as COPC, WAC mosaic | `*` | yes | Yes, through a VRT |
| `stac.astrogeology.usgs.gov/api` | STAC for all of the above | yes | n/a | The index the Kaguya VRT is built from |
| `cartocdn-gusc.global.ssl.fastly.net` (OPM Moon basemap) | Shaded relief, Web Mercator XYZ | `*` | n/a | Basemap, true position |
| `trek.nasa.gov` (Moon Trek WMTS) | WAC and other mosaics | `*` | n/a | Geographic tile matrix, not Web Mercator |
| `pgda.gsfc.nasa.gov` | Barker 2023 polar caps 60°S to 83°S, 240 to 10 m COGs | no | yes | titiler, or a re-host |
| `pds-geosciences.wustl.edu`, `imbrium.mit.edu` | LOLA GDR (raw IMG, JP2) | no | yes | None |

Neither S3 bucket sends `Access-Control-Expose-Headers`, so a browser hides `Content-Range`. geotiff.js 2.1.3 only reads the file size from it, and the single-range path works without it; the Node check above ran with the header removed.

The scale factor matters for the strip TIFFs: the 118 m and SLDEM2015 files store half-metres (`Scale 0.5` in their metadata), and the VRT reader ignores scale and offset, so a VRT over them must not be used as is. A COG repack would write metres.

## How the first exploration maps onto main

| August 2026 plan | Now | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `stacdem://`, a protocol of its own: base COG below a zoom, Kaguya strips warped over it above, through `geowarp` and proj4 | Dropped. `vrt://` already intersects, reads and reprojects with proj4, with an approximate transformer, refusals that keep the map from stalling, and retries | One reader for every mosaic; nothing lunar in the code |
| FlatGeobuf tile index of every Kaguya strip, built in CI, bbox-queried by range requests | `docs/scripts/build-kaguya-vrt.mjs` writes a VRT over a region straight from the STAC items (`proj:shape` and `proj:transform`, no header reads), kept on the dataset-indexes release like Scotland's | A VRT is the index format the reader and GDAL both open |
| CI workflow re-hosting LOLA global and the PGDA caps as 3857-labelled COGs on Hugging Face | Not needed for the 87°S cap (USGS re-hosts it as a COG with CORS) or for the projections (`a_srs`, true position). Still needed for a global LOLA or SLDEM2015 layer | The USGS global files are strip TIFFs; see the follow-ups |
| Three Mercator tricks done with `gdalwarp` | Two, done by the reader: native lunar CRS (true position) and `?a_srs=EPSG:3857` (metric fake-geo) | No file is rewritten |

The Kaguya VRT is written in lunar equirectangular with clon = 0 rather than the files' own clon = 180: on a sphere that is a shift of πR in x, so every strip keeps its pixels and only its `DstRect` moves, and the seam goes from the middle of the nearside to the 180th meridian.

```bash
node docs/scripts/build-kaguya-vrt.mjs --bbox 2,24,6,28 --name apollo15   # 72 strips, 42 KB, about 15 m
pnpm datasets:push public/vrt/kaguya-tc-dtm-apollo15.vrt                  # then commit datasets.lock.json
gdalinfo /vsicurl/https://terrain-viewer.iconem.com/vrt/kaguya-tc-dtm-apollo15.vrt
```

A whole-Moon Kaguya VRT would list about 118 000 sources, some 70 MB of XML, which is more than the browser should parse. The way past that is the [cell-routed](/dev/cell-routed-sources) pattern: a `kaguya://` URL for which the reader asks the STAC API for the strips under each tile and builds a one-strip VRT per item, exactly as it does for GEM-15 cells. That is the part of `stacdem://` worth keeping.

### Index and data, terrain and imagery

The index says which strips touch a tile, the COGs give the pixels. Four roles:

| Role | Source | Form |
|---|---|---|
| Terrain, coarse and global | Cesium Moon Terrain (token), or a future LOLA 118 m COG | Quantized mesh, or a COG |
| Terrain, detail | Kaguya TC DTM strips, the 87°S 5 m cap | USGS COGs through a VRT |
| Imagery, global | OPM Moon basemap (true position) | Web Mercator tiles |
| Imagery, detail | The strips' `orthoimage_left` COGs | The same VRT machinery, as a basemap |

[deck.gl-raster](https://github.com/developmentseed/deck.gl-raster) reprojects COGs on the GPU and could serve the imagery rows, including polar stereographic ones (see its custom projections work), but a deck.gl layer cannot feed MapLibre's terrain, so elevation stays on `vrt://`.
