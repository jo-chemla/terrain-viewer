The Library holds pre-rendered relief (hillshade, sky view factor, local dominance, see [Pre-rendered relief](/features/basemaps#pre-rendered-relief)) beside raw elevation. This page records a sweep, on 2026-11-25, of the research projects and portals the docs cite ([Related Work](/resources/related-work), [Research References](/resources/research-references)) for tile endpoints of their lidar products. Every endpoint below was asked for one tile or a GetCapabilities with `curl`, with an `Origin` header to see the CORS answer. "No CORS" means the browser cannot read the tiles itself.

## Already in the Library

The archaeology renders the docs cite were already present before this sweep: the twelve MARI-GISLAB (Tulane) services for Tikal, Holmul, La Corona and Chactún (hillshade, SVF, local dominance; PACUNAM Lidar Initiative and CHRP surveys, CC BY-NC-SA), the M-REDD+ and G-LiHT blends, the G-LiHT 2013 Yucatan DTMs (OpenTopography COGs) and the Kiuic KMZs ([NASA G-LiHT](/dev/g-liht)), Aguada Fénix (Inomata, OpenTopography), Flanders's sky view factor, Bavaria's combined relief, and the national hillshades of the [table](/features/basemaps#pre-rendered-relief). NCALM's surveys are published through OpenTopography, which the catalog search browses.

### PACUNAM: is the DTM anywhere?

No, as far as a search on 2026-11-25 finds. The 2016 survey (2 144 km² of the Maya Biosphere Reserve, Petén, Teledyne Optech Titan; Canuto et al. 2018, [Science 10.1126/science.aau0137](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aau0137)) was organised and funded by Fundación PACUNAM, which holds the point clouds, DTM and DSM; later papers use them through the consortium.

- **Dryad** ([10.5061/dryad.k51j708](https://doi.org/10.5061/dryad.k51j708), 25 MB): only the features mapped on the lidar, as shapefiles (structures as points, causeways, defensive works, field zones in uplands and wetlands, reservoirs as a spreadsheet, the survey polygons) and three point-density layers on a 1 km² grid. No elevation raster, no point cloud.
- **OpenTopography**: nothing for the reserve. The catalog over northern Petén (-91.5 to -89° E, 16.8 to 17.9° N, point clouds and rasters, federated included) lists only global DEMs, the G-LiHT 2013 Yucatan transects and the Middle Usumacinta survey (Inomata, Mexico).
- **tDAR**: a collection, "LiDAR in the Maya Heartland: results of the 2016 LiDAR survey in Guatemala's Maya Biosphere Reserve" (collection 65082), holds conference papers and reports about the survey; its pages refuse automated reads, and nothing found says rasters are in it.

So the only tiled views of the PACUNAM lidar remain MARI-GISLAB's renders (Tikal, Holmul, La Corona, hillshade, SVF and local dominance, CC BY-NC-SA, in the Library). The DTM itself would have to be asked of Fundación PACUNAM or the PLI investigators (Tulane's MARI, Estrada-Belli, Canuto).

Also found, not terrain: MARI's Chactún (Campeche, Mexico, CHRP), M-REDD+ and G-LiHT renders, already in the Library; its `*_groups_buffers_tif` and heatmap layers, which are settlement density. MARI publishes no Naachtun, El Tintal, El Zotz or Mirador block and no layer over the whole 2 144 km²: `PLI_polygons_noholes22`, a feature layer, is the only thing that covers the twelve survey blocks. No ImageServer, WCS, COG, Earth Engine asset or PMTiles of the survey turned up, `https://pacunam.org/lidar/` answers 404, and GitHub holds only a link to a MARI FeatureServer. Not searched this time: Esri Hub sites of Guatemalan agencies (CONAP, SEGEPLAN, whose IDEG GeoServer WCS answers with zero coverages), NCALM's own site, and the Earth Engine community catalogue.

So the answer stands with one addition: the PACUNAM survey streams as MARI's renders over three of its twelve blocks, and as an 8-bit greyscale of the Holmul DEM; the elevation values themselves are not served anywhere public. Who to ask: Fundación PACUNAM (which funded and holds the data), the PLI investigators at Tulane's Middle American Research Institute (Marcello Canuto, Francisco Estrada-Belli), and NCALM (University of Houston, Juan Carlos Fernandez-Diaz), which flew and processed it.

Could the app show it? Imagery in Web Mercator on Mars coordinates (OpenPlanetaryMap) would load as a plain basemap today; the map just labels Earth places. The rest needs three pieces:

- **Relabel, not reproject.** PROJ refuses transforms between celestial bodies (unless `PROJ_IGNORE_CELESTIAL_BODY` is set on the server), so titiler cannot warp a Moon COG to Web Mercator. The browser COG reader would have to treat the file's lunar lon/lat (or its equirectangular metres divided by the lunar radius) as Earth lon/lat.
- **Scale.** On Earth's tiles a lunar degree is drawn 3.67 times too long (6 378 / 1 737.4 km; 1.88 for Mars), so slopes, hillshade and shadows come out flattened by that factor. An exaggeration of 3.67 (Mars 1.88) restores true slopes; contour intervals and heights stay in metres.
- **Grid.** Trek tiles are equirectangular, not Web Mercator: they need a reprojecting protocol like `epsg3395://` (lib/epsg3395-protocol.ts) but from the plate carrée grid. Web Mercator also stops at 85°, which cuts the lunar south pole where the Artemis sites are.

The most promising route for heights is the USGS STAC: Float32 COGs with CORS, already a STAC API like the presets of [STAC search](/dev/stac-search), once the reader relabels their CRS.
