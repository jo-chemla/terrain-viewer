Two QGIS plugins are released, both in [`qgis-plugin/`](https://github.com/Iconem/terrain-viewer/tree/main/qgis-plugin), their zips on the repository's `qgis-plugins-*` releases ([install](#install)):

- **Terrain Viewer** (0.7.0, MIT, QGIS 3.28 to 4.x): a dock panel laid out as the app's sidebar. A backend is picked at its top: the **local service** (a [tile service](/dev/tile-service) packaged for QGIS, downloaded and started from the panel), a **remote service** (any tile service's address), or **Python** (experimental: the modes computed in QGIS itself with numpy, no service at all, [QGIS Live Compute](/dev/qgis-live-compute)). Under all three, the same modes tree: each checked mode is a layer, with its settings in the layer's address.
- **Send2MapApp** (0.5.1, GPL v2 or later, QGIS 3.22 to 4.x): "Open in", the place shown or clicked in QGIS opened in another viewer at the same place and scale ([below](#send2mapapp-open-in)).

Both are checked headless in QGIS 4.2 and 3.44 LTR (`qgis-plugin/test_*.py`). Neither is on plugins.qgis.org yet. How they came about: [design history](#design-history).

## Terrain Viewer: the panel

A dock panel laid out as the app's sidebar. From the top:

1. **The backend**: three buttons, **Local service**, **Remote service**, **Python**, and under them the picked one's options (below).
2. **The status line**: what is connected, or why not.
3. **Source**: the terrain source (from the service's `/terrain-sources`), and a **zoom to** button that moves the map canvas to the source's bounds (the whole world for a global source such as Mapterhorn).
4. **API keys**, folded behind a key icon: Mapbox, MapTiler, HERE, Google, Cesium ion. They are kept in QGIS's settings (`terrain_viewer/mapboxKey`, `maptilerKey`, `hereKey`, `googleKey`, `cesiumIonKey`). The three a tile service reads (`mapboxKey`, `maptilerKey`, `cesiumIonKey`) go into the address of every layer, so a project saved with a keyed source holds the key; the Python backend uses the Mapbox and MapTiler keys for those two sources. HERE and Google are the app's basemap keys: kept, sent nowhere yet.
5. **Open link**: a share link, whole or just its query.
6. **Freeze**.
7. **Light**, folded: the light of hillshade, Phong, terrain shadows and building shadows, one for all as in the app. A pad (drag the point: its angle from north, clockwise, is the azimuth; its distance from the centre the elevation, 90° at the centre, 0° at the rim) and two sliders, kept in step.
8. **The modes tree**: the app's groups (Elevation Hypso, Terrain Analysis, Relief Visualization, Hillshade, Lighting Effects, Overlays).

### The three backends

| Backend | What it is | When to use it | Its options |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Local service** | The tile service packaged for QGIS (Node, the service, its image libraries; 48 to 59 MB per platform), run on this machine (`127.0.0.1`) from the plugin's data folder | No desktop app, no server to run: every mode and source, nothing to set up | **Connect** (installs it the first time, then starts it; about 3 s), **Install** / **Remove** |
| **Remote service** | Any tile service at an address: the [desktop app's](#with-the-desktop-app) (`http://localhost:3400`), one you run (`cd tile-server && pnpm start`, e.g. `http://localhost:3400`), or a hosted one | The desktop app is open, you develop the service, or a team shares one | The address, **Connect** |
| **Python** (experimental) | The modes computed in QGIS's own Python with numpy and served from a thread of QGIS on `127.0.0.1` ([QGIS Live Compute](/dev/qgis-live-compute)) | No service at all, fast panning; for the modes it computes | **Connect** |

- **Connect** works the same for each: the service is asked `/health` first (3 s), so a service that is not there gives one message in the status line and the message bar, and no layer is made until a backend is connected (no tile asks a dead address). Picking another backend connects to it (the local service once installed: its download waits for Connect): the checked modes' layers are re-pointed to it (their style, opacity and resampling kept), and the local service, if it ran, stops.
- The panel opening (the toolbar button, a saved project) connects a remote service, but never starts the local service or the Python backend: those wait for **Connect**.
- 0.6.0's bug, "with Local tile service checked, Connect does nothing": the button's `clicked(checked=False)` argument reached `connect_service(start_bundled)` as "do not start". Every button now drops it.
- While connected to a service, its `/health` is asked every 15 s in the background; a service that went away is said once, and its return too.

### One modes tree for every backend

The tree comes from the service's `/processes` (each mode as an [OGC API - Processes](/dev/tile-service#visualization-modes-ogc-api---processes) description: its group, form, shading, opacity parameter, settings with their types and defaults). Before any service answers, and for the Python backend, it comes from the snapshot the plugin carries (`assets/catalog.json`, made by `qgis-plugin/make_assets.py` from a tile service), so the tree is there at once and the same under every backend. A mode or parameter added to the app reaches the panel through the service with no plugin release.

- **A mode's row**: its checkbox on the left, its forms on the right, then the settings cog. A click anywhere on the row's line toggles the mode, not only on the box. The forms are **Colour** (the mode as the app draws it, an XYZ layer), **Values** (the numbers before the colour ramp, a Float32 raster GDAL reads from the service's `/gdal.xml`, for the raster calculator, zonal statistics or Identify; only for the modes computed as numbers) and, for the overlays, **Vector** then **Raster**.
- **Overlays default to vector tiles**: checking contours, iso-line, mound candidates, plane slicer or graticule turns on its Vector form (sharp at every zoom, styled in QGIS in the mode's colour); Raster draws it as the app does.
- Checking a mode turns on its default form (the first time; afterwards the forms it had); each form can be checked alone or with the others, and checking one checks the mode. Unchecking the mode or its last form **removes** its layers, every layer with its key (a copy made with QGIS's Duplicate Layer carries the key: 0.6.0 left it drawing), and **the groups left empty go too**, the live group itself when nothing is left. A layer removed by hand unchecks its form.
- **Under the Python backend**, the modes it does not compute are greyed out, with a note on hover; the settings it ignores (the app's colour ramps, for instance: it draws with QGIS's) are greyed out too; and only the forms it makes show, Values only where the mode has values (hillshade is colour only).
- **Settings**, under the cog while a drawn form is on: every number that has a usual range is a **slider with its number box**, as in the app (sun azimuth and elevation, opacities, exaggeration, radii, ranges; the box takes values beyond the slider). **Colour ramps** are a select with each ramp's preview, **matcap textures** a select with each texture's preview (the app's 20), both packaged with the plugin as sprite sheets (`assets/colorramps.png`, `assets/matcaps.png`, made by `make_assets.py` from the service's `/colorramps/{id}.png` and the app's matcap pictures). Colours are QGIS colour buttons.
- **Symmetric ramps** (LRM, TPI, curvature, openness, local dominance, shape index, and the hypsometric tint's "symmetric"): checked, the maximum is edited and the minimum follows it live (minimum = -maximum), read only.
- A changed setting rewrites the layer's address half a second after the last change (`setDataSource`, which keeps the renderer, opacity, blending and resampling set in the layer's properties). Only the settings that differ from the app's defaults go into it.
- **Opacity and blending are QGIS layer settings**: the tiles are asked opaque (QGIS draws neighbouring tiles a pixel over each other, so half transparent tiles show lines along their edges); the shading modes (hillshade, Phong, terrain and building shadows) are multiplied over the layers under them.
- Each form is a layer of the "Terrain Viewer (live)" group, in a subgroup named as the app's panel group, stacked as the map stacks them; the Python backend's layers go in the same tree (0.6.0 had a separate "local compute" group, removed on connect).

**Sources under the Python backend**: it reads Terrarium and Terrain-RGB tile pyramids, decoded in numpy: Mapterhorn (the default), AWS Terrain, the library's other Terrarium and Terrain-RGB sources with a public address (GSI Japan, LINZ New Zealand, Open Waters Seascape, Re:Earth), Mapbox and MapTiler with their keys, or a typed `{z}/{x}/{y}` address (Terrain-RGB when it says so, Terrarium otherwise). It follows the source picked: changing it re-points the layers. Not read: COGs, VRTs, LERC (Esri), WMS/WCS rasters, quantized mesh (Cesium), PMTiles and the differences of two sources; picking one says so once and draws Mapterhorn. Those need a tile service, which reads every GDAL source (in principle the Python backend could read any GDAL source too, through GDAL's warped reads; not done).

### Open link, Freeze, saved projects

- **Open link**: paste a share link (`https://terrain-viewer.iconem.com/?zoom=...`) or only its query (`zoom=12.34&lat=...`, with or without the `?`); its modes are checked, their settings and opacities set, the terrain source picked, the light set, the canvas moved to its camera. With a service, `/view?<the link's query>` answers what the view switches on (the reading `/qgis.qgz` does); under Python, the link's `show*` switches are read by the panel. The service is asked first: if it is not there, one message, no layer.
- The link's source is kept: `terrainSourceA` (the app's name for view A's source) is read as the source, and an id the list lacks is added to it and picked (0.6.0 typed it into the box, where it was ignored, so the previous source, for instance `custom-at-tirol-dgm5`, stayed in every address).
- The link's other parameters go into every layer's address only when some mode takes them (`/processes`' inputs): the app's own, such as `splitRatio`, the camera or view B's source, stay out of the tiles (0.6.0 forwarded them all).
- **Freeze** copies the layers drawn now (the checked ones) into a new group under the live one, which stays on top, named with the date, the time and the place ("Frozen 2026-11-24 14:05 — 44.51, 4.00 z11"): XYZ, vector tile, values and the Python backend's layers alike, every setting in their addresses, with their opacity, blending, style and order. The copies carry no mark the panel looks for, so later changes leave them as they are; several freezes stack, the newest on top.
- A saved project reopens as it was: on Connect, the panel reads its layers' addresses back into its checkboxes and settings.
- Pure Python on QGIS's own API, no dependency (numpy, which QGIS ships, for the Python backend). QGIS is a flat, north-up client that picks the tiles it loads: the plugin never asks for 3D, tilted, rotated or split views, so the service it talks to needs no browser.

### Install

Terrain Viewer 0.7.0 (the panel described above) is on the [`qgis-plugins-2026-11-25e`](https://github.com/Iconem/terrain-viewer/releases/tag/qgis-plugins-2026-11-25e) release, `terrain_viewer-0.7.0.zip` (about 200 KB with its previews and catalog snapshot), with Send2MapApp 0.5.1; it pins the tile service bundles of `qgis-plugins-2026-11-25d`, which its Install button downloads. Earlier zips are on the [`qgis-plugins-2026-11-25d`](https://github.com/Iconem/terrain-viewer/releases/tag/qgis-plugins-2026-11-25d) release (Terrain Viewer 0.6.0, Send2MapApp 0.5.1), for QGIS 3.22 to 4.x (Terrain Viewer needs 3.28); 0.5.0 stays on [`qgis-plugins-2026-11-25c`](https://github.com/Iconem/terrain-viewer/releases/tag/qgis-plugins-2026-11-25c), 0.4.0 on [`qgis-plugins-2026-11-25b`](https://github.com/Iconem/terrain-viewer/releases/tag/qgis-plugins-2026-11-25b) and 0.3.0 on [`qgis-plugins-2026-11-25`](https://github.com/Iconem/terrain-viewer/releases/tag/qgis-plugins-2026-11-25). Since 0.5.0 Terrain Viewer is **one plain zip** for every platform; the tile service is downloaded from the panel when wanted:

| Asset | Size | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `terrain_viewer-0.6.0.zip` | 60 KB | The plugin, pure Python. Install it in QGIS |
| `terrain-viewer-tile-service-windows-x64.zip`, `-macos-arm64`, `-macos-x64`, `-linux-x64` | 48 to 59 MB each | The local tile service of one platform (Node 22, the service, its node_modules with that platform's sharp binaries). Not installed by hand: the plugin's **Install** button downloads the one it pins |
| `service_pins.json`, `SHA256SUMS` | | The bundles' names, sizes and sha256, as the plugin pins them |
| `send2map_app-0.5.1.zip` | 27 KB | Pure Python, no service |

In QGIS: **Plugins > Manage and Install Plugins > Install from ZIP**, pick the zip, **Install Plugin**. The Terrain Viewer button is on the Web toolbar (or **Web > Terrain Viewer**). Not yet on plugins.qgis.org (its `metadata.txt` needs an `email` first). A small pure-Python zip is also the form plugins.qgis.org is built around (one zip for every platform, reviewed as source); the 0.3 and 0.4 platform zips, 48 to 60 MB with a Node executable inside, were for the release only.

#### With the desktop app

The simplest local setup: run the [Terrain Viewer desktop app](/features/advanced/desktop#local-tile-service), turn on **Settings > Desktop: local tile service** (port 3400 by default), then in QGIS open the Terrain Viewer panel with the address `http://localhost:3400` (the default) and press **Connect**. The desktop app runs the same service the bundles carry, on this computer only, for as long as the app is open (and again at its next launch while the setting stays on). With another port, type `http://localhost:<port>` in the panel.

#### The local tile service, installed from the panel

The **Local service** backend, picked at the top of the panel: a line saying what is installed, an **Install** (or **Remove**) button and **Connect**. Connect installs it the first time, then starts it.

- **Install (48 MB)** downloads the bundle of this platform (Windows x64, macOS arm64 or x64, Linux x64) from the GitHub release named in the plugin's `service_pins.json`, checks its sha256 and size against the pin (a different file is refused and nothing is installed), and unpacks it into the plugin's data folder in the QGIS profile, `<profile>/terrain_viewer/tile-service/<release>/<platform>/`. The download goes through QGIS's network manager (its proxy and SSL settings), in a QGIS task with its progress in the status line; GitHub's redirect to its download host is followed. A plugin version pins one release of the service, so the plugin and the service it starts are always a tested pair; a new pin installs next to the old one, which is then deleted. **Remove** stops and deletes it.
- It runs only when asked for: picking **Local service** (or **Connect** with it picked) starts it; the panel opening (the toolbar button, a saved project) never does, the status line then says to press Connect. Picking another backend stops it.
- **Connect** starts it as a subprocess, `bin/node server.mjs` in that folder, listening on this machine only (`127.0.0.1`), on port 3410 or, when that is taken, a free one; the address field shows it and the status line says where it runs and its process id. The first start takes about 3 s. A saved project's layers follow the port: on Connect their addresses are rewritten with the current one.
- It stops when the plugin unloads (QGIS quitting, the plugin disabled, reinstalled or updated) or another backend is picked; and, if QGIS goes without stopping it (a crash), by itself: it is started with `TV_EXIT_WITH_STDIN=1` and stops when its standard input, a pipe from QGIS, closes. Its log is `terrain-viewer-qgis-tile-service.log` in the system's temporary folder; the status line names it when the service does not start.
- **No Earthdata token in the plugin** (0.6.0; 0.5.0 had a field): a NASA Earthdata token for G-LiHT's protected files lives in a tile service's environment only (`EARTHDATA_TOKEN`). The service started from the panel inherits QGIS's environment, so an `EARTHDATA_TOKEN` set for QGIS reaches it; the plugin never stores one (0.6.0 deletes the old `terrain_viewer/earthdataToken` setting), no token goes into a layer's address or ships in a bundle, and the service reads no `.env` (`TV_ENV_FILE` points at nothing). The service's Earthdata proxy is unchanged.
- **No browser engine**: the service draws what QGIS asks for, flat north-up tiles, with the direct engine (Node and sharp). playwright-core is not in the bundle and the service runs with `TV_NO_BROWSER=1`, so it never looks for Chrome or Edge; a 3D, tilted, rotated or split view, or a source only the app draws, answers 501 with a message.
- If it stops while connected (a crash, the process killed), the background health check warns, and Connect starts it again.
- Zip extraction drops the executable bits on macOS and Linux: the plugin sets them on `bin/node` before starting it. The macOS Node is the one nodejs.org signs and notarizes; sharp's libraries come from its npm packages.
- A tile service unpacked as `terrain_viewer/server/` inside the plugin folder (a 0.4 platform zip, a developer's build with `make_zip.py --server-dir`) is used first, and the button is hidden.

#### How QGIS plugins ship native programs

What the plugin downloads is a Node runtime (the official nodejs.org build, checked against its `SHASUMS256.txt` when the bundle is made), the tile service's JavaScript (`tile-server/*.mjs` and the app's code bundled into `generated/`), and its production node_modules, among them sharp's prebuilt native libraries (libvips) for that platform. It then runs it as a local HTTP service on `127.0.0.1`, which QGIS reads as XYZ and GDAL layers like any web service. Other plugins that need native code have done it three ways:

- **Download on demand.** The classic [WhiteboxTools for QGIS](https://plugins.qgis.org/plugins/wbt_for_qgis) plugin does not include the WhiteboxTools binary: the user downloads it from [whiteboxgeo.com](https://www.whiteboxgeo.com/download-whiteboxtools) or the project's GitHub releases and sets its path in **Settings > Options > Processing > Providers > WhiteboxTools** ([its manual](https://jblindsay.github.io/wbt_book/qgis_plugin.html)); the `whitebox` packages for Python and R download it themselves on first use. Terrain Viewer's **Install** is the second form inside QGIS: one click, a pinned version and a checked hash instead of a manual download and a path to type.
- **Installed with QGIS, the plugin only pointing at it.** GRASS ships with QGIS's Windows and macOS installers and OSGeo4W (a separate package on Linux), and its Processing provider is part of QGIS. SAGA has been a separate plugin since QGIS 3.30 ([Processing Saga NextGen Provider](https://plugins.qgis.org/plugins/processing_saga_nextgen/)): SAGA itself comes from OSGeo4W or SAGA's own downloads, and the plugin is told its folder. The Orfeo ToolBox provider likewise needs OTB installed apart and its folder set in the Processing options. None of these plugins carries the program.
- **Bundled in the plugin.** Rare, because the zip becomes one per platform and large, and a binary in a plugin cannot be reviewed as source. Terrain Viewer 0.3 and 0.4 did it (48 to 60 MB zips, one per platform); 0.5 moved to the download.

What is less common is the form: a local HTTP service rather than a command-line program called per run. It suits tiles, which QGIS already knows how to ask for, cache and cancel; a Processing provider over the same service (one algorithm per mode, writing a GeoTIFF) would be the WhiteboxTools-like form for batch work.

From the repository:

```bash
cd qgis-plugin
python make_assets.py [http://localhost:3400]          # terrain_viewer/assets/: ramp and matcap previews, the catalog snapshot (a tile service running)
python make_zip.py                                     # dist/terrain_viewer-0.7.0.zip and dist/send2map_app-0.5.1.zip
python make_zip.py --service-bundle windows-x64        # dist/terrain-viewer-tile-service-windows-x64.zip (any platform from any host; none named: all four)
python make_zip.py --pins <release-tag> dist           # terrain_viewer/service_pins.json from the bundles in dist/ (before the plain zip)
python make_zip.py --server-dir windows-x64 <folder>   # the service unzipped, as the desktop app ships it
python -I smoke_bundled.py dist/terrain-viewer-tile-service-windows-x64.zip   # starts it as the plugin does, asks a tile, stops it (no QGIS)
# Through the panel, in headless QGIS, from a temporary plugins folder: Install (the bundle served from this machine and
# pinned, a tampered one refused), Connect, processes, a slope layer, a tile, the unresponsive warnings, Remove
QT_QPA_PLATFORM=offscreen python-qgis test_service_paths.py installed dist/terrain_viewer-0.7.0.zip dist/terrain-viewer-tile-service-windows-x64.zip
QT_QPA_PLATFORM=offscreen python-qgis test_service_paths.py installed dist/terrain_viewer-0.7.0.zip   # the real download from the pinned release
QT_QPA_PLATFORM=offscreen python-qgis test_service_paths.py external dist/terrain_viewer-0.7.0.zip http://localhost:3400 [--stop "<command>"]
QT_QPA_PLATFORM=offscreen python-qgis test_panel_units.py   # no service, no network: slider ranges, links, the Python mapping, the assets, the light pad
QT_QPA_PLATFORM=offscreen python-qgis test_plugin.py http://localhost:3400 out/   # the panel against a service: forms, cleanup, both shadows, light, symmetric, previews, links, Freeze
```

`--service-bundle` needs the root install and tile-server's (`tile-server/build.mjs` bundles the app's code into `generated/` first). It takes Node 22.22.3 from nodejs.org, checked against its `SHASUMS256.txt`, and installs tile-server's production dependencies flat (`node-linker=hoisted`: a zip keeps no symlinks) with pnpm's `supportedArchitectures` set to the platform, so the right `@img/sharp-*` binaries land; source maps, type declarations and playwright-core are left out. The [QGIS plugins workflow](https://github.com/Iconem/terrain-viewer/actions/workflows/qgis-plugins.yml) (`.github/workflows/qgis-plugins.yml`, on demand) builds each platform's bundle on its own runner and starts it there with `smoke_bundled.py`; with `release_tag` it attaches the bundles, writes their hashes into `service_pins.json`, builds the plain zips with those pins inside and attaches them too, with `service_pins.json` and `SHA256SUMS`. The pins file is then committed back, so the repository's plugin pins the released bundles. A release: create it (`gh release create qgis-plugins-<date> --prerelease --latest=false`), run the workflow with its tag, commit the attached `service_pins.json`.

## Send2MapApp: "Open in"

A second plugin in the same folder, [`qgis-plugin/send2map_app/`](https://github.com/Iconem/terrain-viewer/tree/main/qgis-plugin/send2map_app): the place shown or clicked in QGIS opened in another viewer at the same place and scale, from a dropdown on the Web toolbar (the current view, in the destination picked, which is remembered), the button itself (a map tool: the point clicked), the map canvas's right-click menu ("Open in" at the point clicked) or **Web > Send2MapApp**. QGIS 3.22 to 4.x; install it from the [`qgis-plugins-2026-11-25c`](https://github.com/Iconem/terrain-viewer/releases/tag/qgis-plugins-2026-11-25c) release as above.

- **A QGIS map tool**: the button is checked while the tool is on, with a crosshair cursor carrying an "open elsewhere" badge, and unchecks itself when another map tool (Pan, Identify, ...) is picked, as QGIS's own tools do. The destination picked is kept in QGIS's settings (`plugins/send2map_app/destination`).
- **Icons**: QGIS-style SVGs for the plugin, Terrain Viewer and the web viewers, QGIS's own 3D globe for Google Maps 3D and Bing Maps 3D, the publishers' logos for Google Earth, Esri, Maxar and Search-EO.

- **Destinations**: every entry of the app's own "Open in" list (Iconem River-REM, Iconem Search-EO, ESRI Wayback, Google Maps 3D, Google Earth historical and 3D on the web, Bing Maps 3D, ShadeMap, LiDAR Lens, BBBike MapCompare), plus Terrain Viewer itself and its historical mode (historical-satellite.iconem.com), Maxar, and Google Earth Pro on the desktop through a KML file. LiDAR Lens is greyed out outside Britain and Ireland, as in the app.
- **The camera**: the canvas centre or the point clicked, the zoom whose 512 px tiles have the canvas's ground resolution there (the app's zoom, so the other viewer opens at the same scale in any project CRS), the heading from the canvas rotation.
- **One list**: the app's destinations are a Python port of `OPEN_IN_DESTINATIONS` (`components/TerrainControlPanel/open-in-links.tsx`). `python qgis-plugin/test_open_in.py` evaluates the TypeScript builders under Node and compares ids, labels, coverage and URLs for a set of cameras, so a destination added to the app fails the test until the plugin has it.
- **Lineage and licence**: it began as [Send2GE (`send2google_earth`)](https://plugins.qgis.org/plugins/send2google_earth/) by Maxim Dubinin and NextGIS ([source](https://github.com/nextgis/qgis_send2google_earth)), a map tool that sends the point clicked to Google Earth through a KML file. Iconem's additions in 2025 (as Send2MapApp: web destinations in a toolbar dropdown) and 2026 (the app's whole list, the right-click menu, the current view, QGIS 4) keep its licence, the GNU GPL v2 or later, and its copyright lines; that is why it is a plugin of its own rather than part of the MIT `terrain_viewer`.

## Design history

Before the plugins, QGIS got the app's modes only through the tile service's `/qgis.qgz`: a project with every mode as an XYZ layer, configured as a view was. A layer's settings lived in its address, and QGIS has no control bound to an XYZ address's parameters, so changing a ramp or a radius meant editing the layer's source URL. A plugin was the way to have controls. The plan (2026-11-23) had two steps, the first cheap:

1. **A panel that writes tile service layers**: written on 2026-11-23; Freeze came in 0.2.0, the "Terrain Viewer (live)" group in 0.2.1, and 0.2.2, the first release (2026-11-24), requires `/processes`. 0.3.0 (2026-11-25) shipped the tile service inside four platform zips of 48 to 60 MB, 0.4.0 made it off by default and dropped the browser engine, and 0.5.0 (the same day) went back to one plain 50 KB zip with the service downloaded by the **Install** button.
2. **The modes computed in QGIS**, with no tile service: the experimental local compute (0.5.0, 16 modes since 0.6.0), below.

### Step 2 as planned

The plan for step 2, kept for reference; what was built and measured is on [QGIS Live Compute](/dev/qgis-live-compute).

- **Reading:** GDAL reads the tiles and nothing else. A Terrarium or Terrain-RGB pyramid through GDAL's WMS/TMS driver (the XML of `gdalTileSource` in `lib/terrain-tile-spec.ts`), a COG or VRT directly, a raw WMS through GDAL's WMS driver. Decoding the RGB packing into metres is numpy (the formulas of `lib/elevation-encoding.ts`). The sources only the app's protocols read (LERC, quantized mesh, GEM-15's cells, a difference) need their own reader or stay on step 1.
- **Computing:** numpy, not `gdaldem`: the app's modes are its own (LRM, sky view factor, openness, matcap, Phong, the mound detector, the eigen-based ones), and the point is that QGIS shows what the app shows. Each `lib/*-protocol.ts` is a kernel over an elevation grid with a margin, which ports to numpy array code: slope, aspect, curvature, TPI, TRI and roughness in a few lines each; sky view factor and openness as the app's ray sweeps (vectorised per direction); matcap and Phong as the shaders' formulas per pixel; the colour ramps from `lib/color-ramps.ts`.
- **Delivery:** a QGIS raster layer per mode, computed for the canvas extent at the canvas resolution and recomputed on pan and zoom (debounced, in a background task), or written to a GeoTIFF on request. Optionally a Processing provider, so each mode is also an algorithm for models and batch runs.
- **Checking:** the port is checked against the app tile by tile, as `tile-server/compare-engines.mjs` checks the direct engine against the browser: same tiles, a table of differences.
- **Keeping in step:** two implementations of each mode drift. The comparison above runs in CI on a fixed set of tiles, so a change to a protocol that the Python port does not follow fails a check.

Where it stands (0.7.0: the Python backend is one of the panel's three, under the same modes tree): reading through GDAL, the numpy kernels, delivery as Float32 values GDAL reads (so QGIS's own colour ramps apply) or as coloured tiles, and the tile-by-tile check are done for 16 modes, Phong and matcap included, plus contour lines as a vector layer per view (`qgis-plugin/test_live.py`, against the app's kernels in Node, the tile service and gdaldem). Not done: the comparison in CI, the Processing provider, and the modes that need a whole view (the mound detector, local dominance, the eigen-based ones); for those, the tile service layers stay the way in.
