[G-LiHT](https://gliht.gsfc.nasa.gov/) is NASA Goddard's airborne LiDAR, Hyperspectral and Thermal imager. Its data center, [glihtdata.gsfc.nasa.gov](https://glihtdata.gsfc.nasa.gov/), is a Leaflet webmap over an Apache directory tree (`/files/G-LiHT/<campaign>/`), with no login. Two parts of it are now in the app: the 2013 Yucatan transects OpenTopography re-hosts as COGs (the two **MEX - G-LiHT 2013 Yucatan** library entries, see [Related Work](/resources/related-work#maya-lowlands-lidar-the-petén-paper-and-its-data)), and, since 2026-11-24, any campaign's own DTM KMZ, read in place by the `gliht://` protocol. Tested on 2026-11-24.

## Verdict

- **The KMZ pictures are elevation, not a hillshade.** Each DTM KMZ is a GDAL super-overlay of PNG tiles whose grey is the 1 m bare earth, linearly stretched: black at (or just above) the raster's minimum, white at its 99.9th percentile. Measured on 2026-11-25 against the Float32 GeoTIFFs of eight campaigns (below), the grey decodes back to the GeoTIFF at the 8-bit step: 0.01 to 0.2 m RMS, 0.9 m on a 750 m mountain. The stretch is per KMZ (every tile of a KMZ gives the same fit) and written nowhere in it, and its low end is not a fixed percentile, so heights need the two numbers measured once against the GeoTIFF; without them the grey is still the relief's shape, up to a scale.
- **The app reads the KMZs in place** (`gliht://`, `lib/gliht-kmz.ts`): the zip's central directory by HTTP Range, then only the PNG tiles of the level a map tile needs, warped from their lon/lat quads to Web Mercator. Any campaign as a greyscale overlay from the coverage popup (**Use as overlay**), and the three Kiuic blocks as **terrain** (heights decoded with their measured stretch) and overlays in the library.
- **CORS**: the host still sends none. By default (since 2026-11-25) the app reads through the tile service's allow-listed proxy on the same computer, `http://localhost:3400/proxy/gliht` (**Settings > API Keys > G-LiHT proxy**, see [Tile service](/dev/tile-service#cors-proxy-for-nasa-g-liht)); when it does not answer, a message says so and NASA's host is read directly, which works with a CORS-unblocking browser extension. Until 2026-11-25 the extension route showed nothing: the reader's first request was a suffix Range, which needs a preflight the extensions do not approve ([below](#cors-why-an-unblocking-extension-showed-nothing)). Set the extension up for this one host, not for every site, and keep it off otherwise: [a CORS extension, one host at a time](#a-cors-extension-one-host-at-a-time).
- **Raw Float32 through Earthdata**: LP DAAC's cloud copy (`GLDTMT`, 22 093 DTM granules) is the same GeoTIFFs as NASA's gzipped files, uncompressed, so they are range-readable, but only with an Earthdata login and with no CORS. The tile service's `/proxy/earthdata` holds the token (`EARTHDATA_TOKEN`, never in the browser) and writes a VRT over a granule for the app's `vrt://` reader. Not a COG: no overviews, so usable from about z16.
- **Canopy height** is in the same packaging (KMZ, gzip, `GLCHMT`), not wired: not asked for.

### Inside a DTM KMZ

`Kuiic1_Apr2013_DTM.kmz` (19 MB, the whole campaign) is a zip of 10 923 entries, all deflated, written by GDAL's KMLSUPEROVERLAY driver. `doc.kml` holds one `NetworkLink` to `0/0/0.kml`; each `L/x/y.kml` holds a `GroundOverlay` (its `L/x/y.png` placed by a `gx:LatLonQuad`, the raster being UTM and so slightly rotated in lon/lat) and four `NetworkLink`s to the next level, gated by `Region`/`Lod` (`minLodPixels` 128). Seven levels, 0 to 6 (1, 4 … 4 096 tiles), each tile a 78 × 203 RGBA PNG: the tiles are fixed pixel blocks of the UTM raster counted from its south-west corner, and the last level is the 1 m raster itself (5 005 × 13 013 there). A tile's PNG and KML are next to each other in the zip, so one Range request reads both. AMIGA-Carb ships one KMZ per cell (`lidar/kml/dtm/<campaign>_<cell>_DTM.kmz`, `…_l0s397_DTM.kmz` is 2.8 MB, 719 entries, levels 0 to 4, 69 × 357 tiles) and lists the cells in `lidar/kml/tiles/<campaign>_tiles.kmz`.

There is no legend, `ScreenOverlay`, `ExtendedData` or description in any KML (GDAL writes an empty `<description>` and a `checkHideChildren` list style, nothing else). The PNGs are grey (R = G = B), alpha 0 off the swath and 255 on it, values 1 to 255. A level-6 tile of a flat area reads 44 to 59, not the ~180 a hillshade gives flat ground, and the level-3 mosaic shows the Puuc hills as bright blobs: elevation.

### The PNGs against the GeoTIFF

Measured on 2026-11-25 with `scripts/gliht-stretch.py` on eight campaigns: the three Kiuic blocks and five others picked for their relief (an Alaskan valley, Puerto Rico's El Yunque, the Algodones dunes, the Shenandoah valley, Long Island's dunes). For each, the whole `<campaign>_DTM.tif.gz` and the KMZ were downloaded (to a scratch folder, never the repository), and every PNG tile of the 1 m level (2 000 pixels a tile) was compared with the GeoTIFF box-averaged over each grey pixel's footprint, placed by the tile's quad, in the swath and away from its edge. Fitted: metres = `lo` + grey / 255 × (`hi` − `lo`), on the pixels below 255.

- **Linear.** A quadratic fit gives the same RMSE to the millimetre on all eight, and the mean residual per band of 51 grey levels is within ±2 cm (±5 cm at El Yunque, whose step is 2.9 m).
- **At the 8-bit step.** The RMSE is the quantization's, step / √12 (0.097 m at Kiuic 1, 0.012 m at Long Island), a little above it on steep 1 m relief (Healy, Harrisonburg), where the grey pixel and the GeoTIFF cell are not the same grid. A coarse level (8 m pixels) gives the same `lo` and `hi` within a few centimetres and a higher RMSE (0.2 m at Kiuic), the averaging inside each pixel: the earlier advice to fit on a coarse level is withdrawn.
- **Per KMZ, not per tile.** Fitted tile by tile, `lo` and `hi` stay within a fraction of the step of the whole-KMZ fit; the tiles share one stretch.
- **White is the 99.9th percentile**, every time (0.09 % to 0.13 % of the GeoTIFF above `hi`; `hi` within 0.25 m of the percentile, 1.4 m at El Yunque whose step is 2.9 m). **Black is not a fixed rule**: the raster's minimum on four (Kiuic 1 and 3, Long Island, the AMIGA cell), between the 0.05th and 0.08th percentile on the others (3.6 m above the minimum at Healy, 2.9 m at El Yunque). Probably percentiles of an approximate (sampled) histogram, where a thin low tail is sometimes kept and sometimes cut. So `lo` = min, `hi` = p99.9 decodes the Kiuic blocks to 0.1-0.3 m but others to 0.6-1.7 m: an offset of the whole surface and a slight scale error.
- **Nothing to derive it from**: no metadata in any KML, nothing in the names, the PNG greys run 1 to 255 in all eight. The webmap's `flightData.js` lists sizes and footprints, no statistics. A stretch for a campaign comes only from its GeoTIFF (or the Earthdata granule).

The Kiuic library entries use the 1 m fits above (Kiuic 1 moved from 67.67 / 153.37 to 67.66 / 153.42, Kiuic 3 from 85.71 / 152.76 to 85.71 / 152.74, Kiuic 2 unchanged): a few centimetres.

**Can a hillshade be inverted?** Not in general: a one-light hillshade keeps only the gradient's component towards the light (the perpendicular one is lost), plus no absolute height and no scale; shape-from-shading gives an approximation, never the DTM. It does not arise here, the pictures are the heights.

The recipe, for another campaign: download its DTM `.tif.gz` (gunzip it; or one member of the `.tar.gz`) and its KMZ, run `python scripts/gliht-stretch.py <c>_DTM.tif <c>_DTM.kmz` (numpy, rasterio, Pillow; seconds to a minute), and take `fit.lo` and `fit.hi`. Without the fit: `hi` = the GeoTIFF's 99.9th percentile, `lo` = its minimum, good to a few decimetres on some campaigns and to a couple of metres on others (table).

### CORS: why an unblocking extension showed nothing

Reported on 2026-11-24: on the deployed site, with an "allow CORS" extension, the Kiuic sources drew nothing, DevTools showing the `OPTIONS` preflight succeed and the real requests fail; through the local proxy they drew. The host is not the cause: `https` with no redirect (no hop to another host that would lose the extension's headers), `206` with `Content-Range` and `Accept-Ranges`. The reader's first request was:

- **A suffix Range**, `bytes=-65557`, for the zip's end record. The Fetch standard lets `Range` through without a preflight only as a simple range, `bytes=N-` or `bytes=N-M`; a suffix range triggers one. The extensions add `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` (and allow-methods) to every answer, but no `Access-Control-Allow-Headers: range` (by default; an MV3 extension cannot echo the request's headers), so the preflight's CORS check fails. DevTools shows the `OPTIONS` row as `200` (its HTTP status, not the check) and the `GET` as a CORS error: it is never sent. Every KMZ's first read died there, so nothing else was asked.
- **Reading `Content-Range`** for the file's length: not a CORS-safelisted response header, so hidden unless exposed (`Access-Control-Expose-Headers`, which the extensions do not send by default). It returned null, which only cost an extra request, but it was the next thing to fail.

Checked in Chrome 158 (headless) against a local server answering like the extensions (`Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *` on everything, no allow-headers, no expose-headers):

| Request | Preflight | Result |
|---|---|---|
| `Range: bytes=-65557` | yes, refused | `TypeError: Failed to fetch`, the server sees only the `OPTIONS` |
| `Range: bytes=100-199` | none | `206`, 100 bytes, `Content-Range` null, `Content-Length` 100 |
| `Range: bytes=100-` | none | `206` |
| `HEAD` | none | `200`, `Content-Length` readable |
| `bytes=-65557`, preflight answered `Access-Control-Allow-Headers: *` | yes, passes | `206`, `Content-Range` still null |

So since 2026-11-25, on NASA's host itself, `openZip` asks a `HEAD` (`Content-Length` is a safelisted response header) and then the tail as `bytes=<length − 65557>-<length − 1>`; every later read was already `bytes=start-end`; and nothing reads `Content-Range` any more (where the tail sits in the file follows from the end record: it starts at the directory's offset plus its size, minus the record's place in the tail). With only `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` added, every request is now a simple one. Through a proxy (which allows `Range`) the single suffix request stays. Tested against the real host with the browser's rules applied to the answers (no suffix range, no `Content-Range`): a Kiuic 1 tile at z14 over 20.09, -89.56 in 5 requests, 39 197 pixels, 82.8 to 153.4 m; `lib/gliht-kmz.test.ts` replays the same rules offline. Not tried with the extension itself in a real browser.

### A CORS extension, one host at a time

A CORS-unblocking extension is a blunt tool. Switched on with its defaults, it adds `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *` to the answers of every site, and that already breaks sites that send cookies or other credentials across origins: the browser refuses a credentialed answer whose allowed origin is `*`, so logins, embedded widgets and APIs on unrelated sites can fail for as long as it is on. Three habits keep it harmless:

- **One rule per host, not a global switch.** Use an extension that adds response headers for a URL filter (a CORS extension that takes a list of sites, or a header editor such as Requestly or ModHeader), and add a rule for `glihtdata.gsfc.nasa.gov` only:

  ```text
  URL filter:  https://glihtdata.gsfc.nasa.gov/*
  Response headers:
    Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *
    Access-Control-Allow-Headers: range
    Access-Control-Expose-Headers: content-range, content-length
  ```

  `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` is what lets the page read the answer. `Access-Control-Allow-Headers: range` lets a `Range` request through its preflight: since 2026-11-25 the reader sends only simple ranges, which need none, but a suffix range or another reader would ([above](#cors-why-an-unblocking-extension-showed-nothing)). Exposing `Content-Range` costs nothing. NASA's host sets no cookies, so `*` is safe there.
- **Another host gets its own rule** when another source needs one, rather than widening the first rule to every site.
- **Keep the extension off when you do not need it.** The tile service's proxy (**Settings > G-LiHT address**, [Tile service](/dev/tile-service#cors-proxy-for-nasa-g-liht)) needs no extension at all, and LP DAAC's Earthdata copy cannot be read through an extension anyway: it wants a token, which only the proxy adds.

### The tiles KMZ

`<campaign>_tiles.kmz` (2.7 kB) is one KML of `Placemark`s, one per cell (`l0s397`, `c0r0`…): its centre point and its footprint polygon. The protocol reads it for the per-cell campaigns, to open only the cell KMZs a tile touches.

### Inside the GeoTIFF archives

The first 30 MB of `AMIGACarb_Yuc_Norte_GLAS_Apr2013_DTM.tar.gz` (286 MB) list and extract three members: one GeoTIFF per cell, `…_l0s397_DTM.tif` (25.7 MB), `…_l0s398_DTM.tif` (58.1 MB). `gdalinfo`: Float32 elevation, WGS 84 / UTM 15N, 1 m pixels, 1118 × 5720, uncompressed, stripped (`Block=1118x1`), no overviews, NaN off the swath. Campaigns without a KML folder ship one `<campaign>_DTM.tif.gz` mosaic instead (Kiuic 1: 102 MB, 5005 × 13013, UTM 16N). Gzip has no index: reaching byte N means inflating everything before it, so neither a browser nor titiler (`/vsigzip/`) reads a window of it. Next to the DTM, `geotiff/` holds `_CHM`, `_aspect`, `_slope`, `_rugosity`, `metrics/` and `lidar_refl/` in the same packaging, and `las/` one `.las.gz` per cell.

## The gliht:// protocol

```
gliht://<campaign>/{z}/{x}/{y}                 the grey picture (raster overlay, type tms)
gliht://<campaign>/{z}/{x}/{y}?dem=<lo>,<hi>   Terrarium heights (terrain, type terrarium)
gliht://<campaign>/{z}/{x}/{y}?cells=1         a per-cell campaign (AMIGA-Carb ones are by default)
```

`lib/gliht-kmz.ts` does the work, with no DOM (fetch and the PNG decoder are passed in), so `lib/gliht-kmz.test.ts` runs it in Node on recorded subsets of real KMZs (`lib/fixtures/gliht/`, 230 kB) and checks ten decoded heights against the Kiuic 2 GeoTIFF (worst 0.14 m). `lib/gliht-protocol.ts` is the MapLibre side.

1. **Directory**: the last 64 kB of the KMZ (`Range: bytes=-65557`) hold the zip's end record; the central directory follows in the same answer when small, else in one more request (640 kB for Kiuic 1's 10 923 entries). Kept per KMZ.
2. **Root**: `0/0/0.kml` and `0/0/0.png` in one request give the raster's quad and the tile size.
3. **Level**: the one whose pixel is no larger than the map tile's (the finest, about 1 m, from z17).
4. **Tiles**: the map tile's corners mapped into the root quad give the columns and rows of that level that can touch it; those present in the directory are read, PNG and KML together, neighbours merged into one Range request.
5. **Warp**: each output pixel's lon/lat is located in each tile's quad (inverse bilinear) and sampled bilinearly, nearest at the swath edge. Off the data: transparent (overlay), or alpha 254 at height `lo` (terrain, the app's nodata convention).

Measured through the proxy: a first Kiuic 1 tile at z16 takes 9 requests and 0.9 MB (most of it the directory, once), its neighbours one request each; an AMIGA cell tile at z15, 5 requests and 1.2 MB.

**Where it reads from** (**Settings > API Keys > G-LiHT proxy**, local, `glihtBaseUrl` in storage):

- **By default, `http://localhost:3400/proxy/gliht`**, the tile service on the same computer (`cd tile-server && pnpm start`). The deployed app is `https`, but `http://localhost` is a potentially trustworthy origin (Secure Contexts), so Chrome, Edge and Firefox do not block it as mixed content. Chrome 142+ asks once for **local network access** (a public site reaching the computer itself); refused, the requests fail like a stopped service. The proxy's preflight answer also carries `Access-Control-Allow-Private-Network: true`, for browsers still on Private Network Access. Safari was not checked. This follows the standards; it was not tried on the deployed site from here (the agent browser draws no map).
- **When the proxy does not answer** (not started, access refused): a message says so once, and the tiles are read from NASA's host directly, the proxy being tried again 30 s later. With a CORS-unblocking extension the map then fills; without one a second message says to start the service or turn on an extension. No silent empty tiles.
- **Empty** (the field cleared, or its **clear** link): NASA's host only, which needs a CORS-unblocking extension, set up for that host only ([how](#a-cors-extension-one-host-at-a-time)).
- Another tile service's `<server>/proxy/gliht` (a link fills it from the Tile Service field), or any CORS proxy written as a template with `{url}` (`https://proxy.example/?url={url}`, the full glihtdata address goes in, encoded).

**In the app**:

- **Coverage > 3D and LiDAR coverage > NASA G-LiHT airborne LiDAR**, click a campaign, **Use as overlay**: the campaign's KMZ as a greyscale overlay (any of the campaigns with a DTM KMZ). Black is its lowest ground, white about its highest.
- **Library**, terrain: **MEX - G-LiHT 2013 Kiuic 1, 2, 3 … NASA KMZ decoded**: the three wall-to-wall Kiuic blocks (93 km², Puuc) as terrain, with the stretches above, so every relief mode works on them. The surface is terraced at the grey step (0.34, 0.21, 0.26 m), visible as faint contour-like bands in a strong hillshade.
- **Library**, basemaps (Pre-rendered relief): the same three as greyscale overlays.

## Earthdata Cloud (LP DAAC)

CMR (open, no login) lists the G-LiHT collections in `LPCLOUD`: `GLDTMT` (DTM), `GLDSMT` (DSM), `GLCHMT` (CHM), `GLDTMK` and `GLCHMK` (the KMZs), `GLMETRICS`, `GLORTHO`, `GLLIDARPC` (point clouds), `GLTRAJECTORY` and the hyperspectral ones. `GLDTMT` (`C2763264722-LPCLOUD`) has 22 093 granules: one per campaign for the mosaic campaigns (`GLDTMT_Kuiic1_Apr2013_DTM`, 248.651 MB), one per cell for AMIGA-Carb (`GLDTMT_AMIGACarb_Yuc_Norte_NFI_Apr2013_l3s455_DTM`). Each granule is one file:

```
https://data.lpdaac.earthdatacloud.nasa.gov/lp-prod-protected/GLDTMT.001/<granule>/<granule>.tif
s3://lp-prod-protected/GLDTMT.001/<granule>/<granule>.tif      (us-west-2, temporary keys from /s3credentials)
https://data.lpdaac.earthdatacloud.nasa.gov/lp-prod-public/GLDTMT.001/<granule>/<granule>.1.png   (browse, public)
```

Kiuic 1's granule is 248.651 MiB, exactly the 260 729 434 bytes of the GeoTIFF inside NASA's `Kuiic1_Apr2013_DTM.tif.gz`: the same file, uncompressed. So it is range-readable over HTTPS, but not a COG (stripped, one row per strip, no overviews): a window costs its rows' full width, and a low zoom costs the whole file.

Access: Earthdata's download app (TEA) accepts an Earthdata **user token** as `Authorization: Bearer <token>` and answers with a redirect to a signed S3 address, valid an hour, which takes Range requests. Without a token, or with a wrong one, it redirects to the login page (a `302`, not a `401`), and it answers a CORS preflight `405`: a browser can never send the token, and must never hold it anyway. Direct S3 (`s3credentials`) works only from AWS us-west-2.

So the tile service does it (`tile-server/proxy.mjs`): `/proxy/earthdata/<path>` adds the token from its environment, follows the redirect without it, keeps the signed address 30 minutes and streams the bytes with CORS; `/proxy/earthdata/vrt/<path>.tif` reads the GeoTIFF's header through that route and writes a VRT whose sources are 512 px windows of it, which the app's `vrt://` reader opens in the browser (custom terrain source, type VRT). The windows make the reader's minimum zoom about 16, where a tile reads a few megabytes of rows; below that it refuses rather than read 250 MB. Tested without a token: a fake TEA and S3 in `tile-server/proxy-test.mjs` (the token never reaches S3, the login redirect becomes a `401`, the signed address is reused), and against the real host: `401` with what to set, the public browse PNG through.

**Setting it up**: generate a token at [urs.earthdata.nasa.gov](https://urs.earthdata.nasa.gov/) (**Generate Token**, valid 60 days), put `EARTHDATA_TOKEN=<token>` in the main checkout's `.env`, start the tile service, and add a custom terrain source of type VRT with the address `http://localhost:3400/proxy/earthdata/vrt/lp-prod-protected/GLDTMT.001/GLDTMT_Kuiic1_Apr2013_DTM/GLDTMT_Kuiic1_Apr2013_DTM.tif`. Step by step, and the check command: [Tile service](/dev/tile-service#getting-the-earthdata-token). For the Kiuic blocks the decoded KMZ is lighter (it has overviews) and within 0.1-0.2 m; the Earthdata route gives the exact Float32 and any of the 22 093 granules.

## The catalog

The webmap reads one index, `DATA/flightData.js` (23 MB: `var flightData = {…}` with, per campaign, the date, the bounds, the file sizes and the corner quad of every cell). `scripts/build-gliht-coverage.mjs` reads it in Node (no CORS there), as JSON text, and writes `public/coverage/gliht.geojson` (5.6 MB, on the `dataset-indexes` release, rebuilt monthly with the other footprints, see [Coverage Footprints](/dev/coverage-footprints)).

- **1 521 campaigns, 68 850 cells, 2011 to 2026**, about 184 000 km² of cell footprints (overlapping repeat flights counted each time). 142 GB of DTM GeoTIFF, 74 GB of CHM, 36 GB of DTM KMZ.
- By region: Alaska 470 campaigns (120 000 km²), AMIGA-Carb Mexico 154 (37 600 km²), Florida 164, Puerto Rico 291, Maine 74, then New Mexico, the Mid-Atlantic, Oregon, the Bahamas (Andros), Virginia, Maryland and a long tail.
- **Strips and blocks.** A cell named `lNsM` is segment M of flight line N, a strip a few hundred metres to a couple of kilometres wide. A cell named `cXrY` is a 1 km tile of a block flown wall to wall. 210 campaigns are blocks (10 100 km²): forest plots, watersheds, Puerto Rico's El Yunque and Rio Abajo, the Jemez and Sandia mountains, Big Pine Key, the Algodones dunes.
- **Wall-to-wall in the Maya lowlands: Kiuic.** `Kuiic1_Apr2013`, `Kuiic2_Apr2013` and `Kuiic3_Apr2013` (the folder names misspell Kiuic) are 93 km² of 1 km blocks over the Kiuic and Bolonchén district of the Puuc, Yucatan, flown in April 2013 with the AMIGA-Carb transects. They are not in the OpenTopography reprocessing (its VRTs list only the `Yuc_*`, `Campeche`, `Chiaps1` and `Out_Yuc` transects); the app reads them from NASA's KMZs (library) or, with a token, from LP DAAC. The same holds for `Hildalgo_May2013` (44 km², Hidalgo) and every AMIGA-Carb flight outside the Yucatan (Oaxaca, Chiapas, Guanajuato, Chihuahua, Sonora…), which the coverage popup can draw as overlays.
- The 2013 Yucatan transects that OpenTopography does carry are 13 campaigns, about 6 400 km² of cell footprints: 3.6 GB of NASA DTM (superseded there by the 0.5 m reprocessing) and **2.2 GB of CHM** (not on OpenTopography).

## Still open

1. **A stretch for every campaign**: heights need `lo`/`hi` per KMZ, measured on the GeoTIFF (`scripts/gliht-stretch.py`); no rule replaces it (black is not a fixed percentile). Doing it for all 1 521 means downloading 142 GB of gzip once (or reading the uncompressed granules through Earthdata); a script could then bake `lo`/`hi` into `gliht.geojson`, and the popup could offer **Use as terrain** too.
2. **Re-hosting as COGs** (Kiuic: 135 MB of gzip in, a few hundred MB out; the Yucatan CHM: 2.2 GB) still gives the best reading: exact Float32 with overviews, no proxy, no token. Not done without approval.
3. **titiler** does not help with the archives (`/vsigzip/` re-inflates from the start for every window); with `GDAL_HTTP_HEADERS="Authorization: Bearer …"` a self-hosted titiler could read the LP DAAC granules, which a public titiler cannot (the token would be in every request).

## Related work and references

Where G-LiHT's data and its description live, and what the app reads from each (links checked 2026-11-25):

- **[G-LiHT](https://gliht.gsfc.nasa.gov/)** (NASA Goddard): the instrument and its campaigns.
- **[G-LiHT Data Center](https://glihtdata.gsfc.nasa.gov/)**: the webmap and the open directory tree. The coverage footprints are built from its `flightData.js` index, and the `gliht://` protocol reads its DTM KMZs.
- **[G-LiHT Digital Terrain Model V001 (GLDTMT)](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/catalog/lpcloud-gldtmt-001)** (LP DAAC, NASA Earthdata): the cloud copy of the DTM GeoTIFFs, bare-earth heights on the EGM96 geoid, which the tile service's `/proxy/earthdata` route reads with a token. [Earthdata Search](https://search.earthdata.nasa.gov/) finds the granules of a place by hand, with the same login.

Papers:

- B. Cook, L. Corp, R. Nelson, E. Middleton, D. Morton, J. McCorkel, J. Masek, K. Ranson, V. Ly, P. Montesano, [NASA Goddard's LiDAR, Hyperspectral and Thermal (G-LiHT) Airborne Imager](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs5084045), *Remote Sensing* 5(8), 2013. The instrument, its data processing and its products, among them the lidar terrain, surface and canopy height models: the terrain model is what the app reads (as KMZ pictures or GeoTIFFs); the canopy height is not wired.
