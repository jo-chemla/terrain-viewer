[G-LiHT](https://gliht.gsfc.nasa.gov/) is NASA Goddard's airborne LiDAR, Hyperspectral and Thermal imager. Its data center, [glihtdata.gsfc.nasa.gov](https://glihtdata.gsfc.nasa.gov/), is a Leaflet webmap over an Apache directory tree (`/files/G-LiHT/<campaign>/`), with no login. The app already streams one part of it: the 2013 Yucatan transects, which OpenTopography re-hosts as COGs (the two **MEX - G-LiHT 2013 Yucatan** library entries, see [Related Work](/resources/related-work#maya-lowlands-lidar-the-petén-paper-and-its-data)). This page is the test of everything else, done on 2026-11-24.

## Verdict

- **No direct client-side route.** The host sends no `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` header on anything (KMZ, tar.gz, the index), for a GET with an `Origin` or for an `OPTIONS` preflight. A page on another origin cannot read a byte, so a custom protocol in the browser is out, whatever the format.
- **The elevation is only in gzip.** Range requests work, but the GeoTIFFs are inside `.tar.gz` (AMIGA-Carb and the newer "KML folder" campaigns) or `.tif.gz` (the rest). Gzip has no index: reaching byte N means inflating everything before it, so neither a browser nor titiler (`/vsigzip/`, `/vsitar/`) can read a window without streaming the archive from the start.
- **The KMZ files are pictures, not elevation.** Each is a regionated super-overlay of greyscale hillshade PNGs. A protocol could drape them through a CORS proxy, but the app already draws a better hillshade, and every other mode, from real elevation.
- **NASA's other copy is behind a login.** LP DAAC holds the same products in the cloud (`GLDTMT` DTM, `GLCHMT` CHM, `GLDSMT` DSM, `GLDTMK`/`GLCHMK` KML, 22 093 DTM granules), at `data.lpdaac.earthdatacloud.nasa.gov/lp-prod-protected/`: an anonymous GET answers 302 to `urs.earthdata.nasa.gov`, and an `OPTIONS` preflight answers 405, so a browser cannot send an Earthdata token either. CMR search itself is CORS-open, the data is not.
- **What was built**: the whole catalog as a coverage overlay (**Coverage > 3D and LiDAR coverage > NASA G-LiHT airborne LiDAR**), so the app shows where G-LiHT exists, what each campaign holds and whether it can be streamed (only the OpenTopography Yucatan transects can).
- **Canopy height still needs re-hosting.** G-LiHT's CHM is in the same gzip archives, OpenTopography publishes the Yucatan bare earth only (no `_hh` or DSM VRT under `MX13E_Britton/` or `MX13W_Britton/`), so there is no DSM to derive an nDSM from in the browser. Meta/WRI's 1 m canopy height (in the library) covers the same ground as a modelled substitute, not a LiDAR one.

## The tests

| What | Request | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| CORS on a DTM KMZ, the tiles KMZ, a DTM tar.gz | `GET` with `Origin:` and `Range: bytes=0-99` | `206 Partial Content`, `Accept-Ranges: bytes`, no `Access-Control-*` header |
| Preflight | `OPTIONS` with `Access-Control-Request-Headers: range` | `200`, `Allow: GET,POST,OPTIONS,HEAD`, no `Access-Control-*` header |
| LP DAAC cloud GeoTIFF | `GET` (with or without a bearer token), `OPTIONS` | `302` to Earthdata Login; `405` |
| The webmap's NCCS ortho MapServer | `maps.nccs.nasa.gov/image02/rest/services/gliht/...` | the host does not resolve |

### Inside a DTM KMZ

`AMIGACarb_Yuc_Norte_GLAS_Apr2013_l0s397_DTM.kmz` (2.8 MB, one strip cell): a zip of 719 entries, all deflated. `doc.kml` holds one `NetworkLink` to `0/0/0.kml`; each level's KML holds a `GroundOverlay` (a `gx:LatLonQuad`, the strip being rotated) and four `NetworkLink`s to the next level, gated by `Region`/`Lod` (`minLodPixels` 128). Five levels, 0 to 4 (1, 4, 16, 64, 264 tiles), each tile a 69 × 357 RGBA PNG; at level 4 that is about 1 m per pixel. The pixels are a greyscale hillshade with transparency outside the swath, not encoded heights. The CHM KMZs are the same layout.

### The tiles KMZ

`<campaign>_tiles.kmz` (2.7 kB) is one KML of `Placemark`s, one per cell (`l0s397`, `l0s398`…): its centre point and its footprint polygon. A footprint index, not imagery.

### Inside the GeoTIFF tar.gz

The first 30 MB of `AMIGACarb_Yuc_Norte_GLAS_Apr2013_DTM.tar.gz` (286 MB) were enough to list and extract three members: one GeoTIFF per cell, `…_l0s397_DTM.tif` (25.7 MB), `…_l0s398_DTM.tif` (58.1 MB). `gdalinfo`: Float32 elevation, WGS 84 / UTM 15N, 1 m pixels, 1118 × 5720, uncompressed, stripped (`Block=1118x1`), no overviews: not a COG, and not tiled. Values 0.6 to 12.5 m there (the coast north of Campeche). Campaigns outside AMIGA-Carb without a KML folder ship one `<campaign>_DTM.tif.gz` mosaic instead (Kiuic 1: 102 MB). Next to the DTM, `geotiff/` holds `_CHM`, `_aspect`, `_slope`, `_rugosity`, `metrics/` and `lidar_refl/` in the same packaging, and `las/` one `.las.gz` per cell.

## The catalog

The webmap reads one index, `DATA/flightData.js` (23 MB: `var flightData = {…}` with, per campaign, the date, the bounds, the file sizes and the corner quad of every cell). `scripts/build-gliht-coverage.mjs` reads it in Node (no CORS there), as JSON text, and writes `public/coverage/gliht.geojson` (5.6 MB, on the `dataset-indexes` release, rebuilt monthly with the other footprints, see [Coverage Footprints](/dev/coverage-footprints)).

- **1 521 campaigns, 68 850 cells, 2011 to 2026**, about 184 000 km² of cell footprints (overlapping repeat flights counted each time). 142 GB of DTM GeoTIFF, 74 GB of CHM, 36 GB of DTM KMZ.
- By region: Alaska 470 campaigns (120 000 km²), AMIGA-Carb Mexico 154 (37 600 km²), Florida 164, Puerto Rico 291, Maine 74, then New Mexico, the Mid-Atlantic, Oregon, the Bahamas (Andros), Virginia, Maryland and a long tail.
- **Strips and blocks.** A cell named `lNsM` is segment M of flight line N, a strip a few hundred metres to a couple of kilometres wide. A cell named `cXrY` is a 1 km tile of a block flown wall to wall. 210 campaigns are blocks (10 100 km²): forest plots, watersheds, Puerto Rico's El Yunque and Rio Abajo, the Jemez and Sandia mountains, Big Pine Key, the Algodones dunes.
- **Wall-to-wall in the Maya lowlands: Kiuic.** `Kuiic1_Apr2013`, `Kuiic2_Apr2013` and `Kuiic3_Apr2013` (the folder names misspell Kiuic) are 93 km² of 1 km blocks over the Kiuic and Bolonchén district of the Puuc, Yucatan, flown in April 2013 with the AMIGA-Carb transects. They are not in the OpenTopography reprocessing (its VRTs list only the `Yuc_*`, `Campeche`, `Chiaps1` and `Out_Yuc` transects), so their elevation is only in the NASA archives: DTM 102 + 13 + 20 MB, CHM 56 + 8 + 12 MB gzipped. The same holds for `Hildalgo_May2013` (44 km², Hidalgo) and every AMIGA-Carb flight outside the Yucatan (Oaxaca, Chiapas, Guanajuato, Chihuahua, Sonora…).
- The 2013 Yucatan transects that OpenTopography does carry are 13 campaigns, about 6 400 km² of cell footprints: 3.6 GB of NASA DTM (superseded there by the 0.5 m reprocessing) and **2.2 GB of CHM** (not on OpenTopography).

## What streaming them would take

The only way in is through a server, once. In order of cost:

1. **Kiuic** (the one wall-to-wall Maya block missing from the app): download `Kuiic{1,2,3}_Apr2013_DTM.tif.gz`, `gdal_translate -of COG` each (deflate, predictor 3, overviews), about 135 MB of gzip in, a few hundred MB of COG out. Host the three COGs on any CORS-open bucket and add a `cog` or `vrt` library entry. Public domain (NASA).
2. **Yucatan canopy height**: the 13 `_CHM.tar.gz` (2.2 GB), each member converted to COG and indexed by one VRT per UTM zone, as the Scotland VRTs are built (see [Cell-routed sources](/dev/cell-routed-sources)). That is the "several GB" of [Related Work](/resources/related-work#maya-lowlands-lidar-the-petén-paper-and-its-data); the test above confirms no shortcut exists.
3. **A CORS proxy** in front of glihtdata (a Cloudflare Worker forwarding `Range` and adding `Access-Control-Allow-Origin`, or `scripts/cors-proxy.mjs` locally) would make the KMZs drawable, but they are hillshades, and the GeoTIFFs would still be gzip. Not worth a protocol.
4. **titiler** does not help: it opens what GDAL can seek, and `/vsigzip/` over HTTP re-inflates from the start for every window.
