Step 2 of the [QGIS plugin plan](/dev/qgis-plugin#step-2-as-planned) asked whether the plugin can draw the app's modes without a tile service, in QGIS's own Python. This experiment (2026-11-25) answers yes: a first round ported three modes of increasing cost and measured them, a second ported eleven more, gave QGIS the values and made the server parallel, a third gave every mode row its Color and Values boxes, added Phong, matcap and contour lines, and measured how QGIS picks tile zooms and resamples. The code is in [`qgis-plugin/terrain_viewer/live/`](https://github.com/Iconem/terrain-viewer/tree/main/qgis-plugin/terrain_viewer/live), behind a panel checkbox that was off by default (**Experimental: compute locally**) until 0.7.0, where it became the panel's **Python** backend, beside the local and remote tile services, under the same modes tree.

## In the panel since 0.7.0: the Python backend

Plugin 0.7.0 dropped the separate box and its own flat list of modes. The **Python backend** is one of the three backends picked at the top of the panel ([the backends](/dev/qgis-plugin#the-three-backends)); since 0.8.0 it starts when picked, with no Connect button, and the panel shows the tile service's modes tree under it, as it does for a service:

- `qgis-plugin/terrain_viewer/python_backend.py` maps the service's vocabulary onto `live/modes.py`'s registry: the mode ids (`colorRelief` is `elevation`; every hillshade method is `hillshade` since its color tile draws the method, before that a multidirectional one was `multihillshade`), the URL parameters (`svfRadius` is `radius`, `contourMinor` is `minor`, `matcapTextureId` is `texture`, ...), and the shared light (`illuminationDir`, `illuminationAlt`) as `azimuth` and `altitude`. The ramp settings keep the service's names (`slopeColorRamp`, `svfMin`, `aspectShiftDegrees`, ...: `live/ramps.py` reads them). It reads the registry's own fields: `python_supported` (the mound detector was greyed out until its port), `service` (the service's id), `outputs` (the forms). A mode added to `live/modes.py` under the service's id is enabled with no change there, its settings matched by name or by their name with the mode's taken off (`localDominanceMaxRadius` is `maxRadius`). All 25 of the tree's modes are computed in Python (24 in 0.7.0).
- The modes `live/` does not compute are greyed out with a note (none left); the settings it ignores too (0.7.0 greyed out the app's color ramps and ranges; they apply since, [below](#parity-with-the-tile-service)); and only the forms it makes show (Values only where the service's mode has values: hillshade is color only). The table of which mode each backend draws is on [the plugin's page](/dev/qgis-plugin#the-modes-and-their-backends).
- Every vector form (contour lines, iso-lines, graticule lines, plane slicer areas) is a memory layer filled for the view by `contours.view_vector` in a QGIS task after each pan or zoom, its geometry and fields as the registry's `vector_layer` declares them (`live/layers.py`, whose layer order is `modes.ORDER`).
- Its layers go in the panel's "Terrain Viewer (live)" tree, keyed as the service's (`slope`, `slope:values`, `contours:vector`), so picking another backend re-points them, Freeze copies them and a saved project reads them back. 0.6.0's "Terrain Viewer (local compute)" group is removed on connect.
- **Sources**: it follows the source picked: Terrarium and Terrain-RGB pyramids (Mapterhorn, AWS, the library's others with a public address, Mapbox and MapTiler with their keys, a typed `{z}/{x}/{y}` address), since the fifth round any elevation GDAL opens ([below](#any-gdal-raster-as-the-source)), and since 0.9.0 the library's COGs, VRTs and raw WMS, WCS and ArcGIS services ([below](#090-the-librarys-raw-elevation-through-gdal)). LERC, quantized mesh, PMTiles and difference sources say so once and draw nothing (0.8.0 drew Mapterhorn in their place). Contour lines and the elevation prefetch follow the source too.
- The hypsometric tint is the app's: its ramp's own stops (mby: -8000 to 5000 m) unless **customHypsoMinMax** is on (then min and max), the range that follows the view (2nd to 98th percentile, read again after each pan or zoom) when **hypsoAutoRange** is on. 0.7.0 drew the plugin's own ramp over the view's range whatever the settings, which is why the user saw a range of 0 to 1 and a ramp the panel could not change: a Values layer made before the canvas had an extent got the renderer's fallback range (0 to 1), and the colour tiles ignored `colorRamp`, `invertColorRamp` and `customHypsoMinMax`.

## 0.9.0: the library's raw elevation through GDAL

The user picked two library sources under the Python backend in 0.8.0: France's IGN LiDAR HD DSM (a WMS answering raw Float32 GeoTIFF) said "the Python backend does not support it", and Canada's NRCan MRDEM (a COG) said the same and then drew the whole world's hillshade from Mapterhorn. Two causes: `/terrain-sources` listed a tile template (`url`) only for plain pyramids, so neither the service nor the plugin's snapshot carried a COG's or a WMS's address; and a refused source fell back to Mapterhorn.

Now `/terrain-sources` lists `address` (with `tileSize` and `nodataFloor`) for every keyless COG, VRT and `wms-raw` entry, the packaged snapshot keeps it, and `live/gdal_source.py library_spec` reads them three ways:

| Kind | Read as | Example (`qgis-plugin/test_sources.py`, network) |
|---|---|---|
| COG, VRT | `/vsicurl/<address>`, probed like any GDAL raster (GDAL's overviews, `GDAL_DISABLE_READDIR_ON_OPEN=EMPTY_DIR`) | NRCan MRDEM (84 GB, EPSG:3979): Mount Robson z13 1824 to 3901 m (the library says 3902 m), probe 2.5 s, a cold tile 3 to 6 s, then 1 ms |
| WMS GetMap of a raw format | a GDAL WMS description (WMS minidriver): the base address, layer, version, CRS and format of the template, a data window over the entry's bounds in EPSG:3857 at its finest resolution, one Float32 band, 512 px blocks, 4 connections, empty blocks for 204, 404 and a service exception; GDAL's own overviews for a zoomed-out view | IGN LiDAR HD DSM: the Eiffel Tower z16 27 to 325 m, Mont Blanc z14 3861 to 4806 m, 2 to 4 s a cold tile |
| WCS, ArcGIS exportImage (no GDAL dataset form) | the app's own request (`float32dem://`): the template's `{bbox-epsg-3857}` filled per source tile with its pixel of margin (514 px for 512), the GeoTIFF answer opened from memory by GDAL and warped onto the tile (a server that answers a larger extent lands right) | AHN (WCS 1.0), Amsterdam z15: -6 to 3 m, its canals no data; 3DEP image service, the Grand Canyon z12: 725 to 2225 m |

The library's `nodataFloor` turns a service's own no data value into holes. A source still not read (LERC, quantized mesh, PMTiles, a difference) draws nothing, with one warning: the panel removes the Python layers of the checked modes until a readable source is picked, instead of drawing another source in its place. The same GDAL address is what the panel's **add raw elevation** button opens as a QGIS layer ([Any GDAL source](/dev/qgis-plugin#any-gdal-source)).

The light pad's live updates came with two measures of the Python backend's server ([the light](/dev/qgis-plugin#terrain-viewer-the-panel)): a 1920x1080 canvas of hillshade at z13 is 40 tiles, computed in about 0.3 s over HTTP from 6 clients (15 ms a tile in one thread, the shading now in float32, the same 8-bit picture: section 2b stays at 100 % within 3 levels, every method), while QGIS's XYZ provider takes 1.0 to 1.3 s to redraw the canvas from them, with HTTP/1.0 or keep-alive alike (keep-alive was tried and measured no faster). And on Windows the server bound its port with `SO_REUSEADDR`, so a second QGIS could bind the port the first listened on and get its tiles answered by the other one ("unknown source"); it binds exclusively now and takes another port.

## Fifth round: parity with the tile service, any GDAL source, the mound detector

After 0.7.0 (2026-11-25), on the user's feedback: the hypsometric tint clamped to 0-1 with a ramp that could not be changed, many settings of the panel not applied under Python, and the question "can I select a local COG from raster layers, or any GDAL source, where GDAL just hands over the pixels of raw elevation that the Python backend transforms?".

### Parity with the tile service

Every colour tile of the Python backend is now the tile service's picture of the same mode and settings, ported rather than approximated:

- **The ramp modes** (colour relief, slope, aspect, TRI, TPI, roughness, curvature, shape index, blobness, eigenvalue ratio, orientation, LRM, sky view factor, openness, local dominance): `live/ramps.py` ports `lib/mode-relief.ts` (which settings colour a mode, with the app's defaults), `computeColorReliefPaint` (the ramp remapped onto min and max, colours swapped end for end when inverted, custom stops held between them when discrete, aspect's cyclic shift, the hole and top stops) and the service's `rampOf` (stops packed in the tile's encoding, colours premultiplied and rounded to 8 bits). The ramps are the app's 268, packaged as `assets/colorramp_stops.json` (`make_assets.py`, from the service's `/colorramps/{id}`). live/'s values are natural units; the ramp reads the app's wire units (blobness, eigenvalue ratio and the sky view factor x100, curvature x100000, shape index x1000, openness minus 90), packed as the app packs them (Terrarium floored to 1/256, slope's Terrain-RGB floored to 0.1) and read as Float32, as the service samples them: a value on a discrete ramp's collapsed pair of stops falls below it, as there.
- **The Values layer** gets the same ramp as a QGIS colour ramp shader in natural units (alpha kept), editable in its properties; it follows the panel's ramp settings when they change.
- **The hillshade**: `live/hillshade.py` ports MapLibre's two passes as the service does (the prepare pass's Sobel sums with the zoom factor of the source's own zoom and tile size, kept in 8 bits; the shading pass with its cos(latitude)), with `computeHillshadePaint`: the eight methods, the shadow, highlight and accent colours, the exaggeration, over the map's white. (Its values stay gdaldem's Horn shade, the iso-line's measure.)
- **Phong**: up to three lights with their colours (`phongLightCount`, `phongLight<i>Color`, `Dir`, `Alt`); **matcap**: the camera-relative switch.
- **Sky view factor and openness**: the "fast" precision, the app's default (radii 1, 2, 4, ... and the near radius within 8 px, one ancestor ring per octave past it, three directions 0.3 rad apart, the steepest); "precise" as before.
- **Contours**: their colour (`rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.5)` by default, as the service's), weight (minor lines half the major's) and the LRM reference (`contourReferenceMode=lrm` with its own intervals).
- **The ground size** of a tile is the one at the centre of the source's own tile (a 512 px Mapterhorn tile at z-1), as the service's protocols take it: the values agree exactly (slope 100 % equal, it was 99.5 % within 0.5 % before).

Measured with `test_live.py --service` (section 2b), Mont Blanc z12 and z13 (z12 to z14 for the pictures), against a service of the same commit and against the user's (the same results): each case is the share of pixels within 3 levels of the service's picture.

### The mound detector

`live/mounds.py` ports `lib/tells-protocol.ts` on the source's own tiles (a 512 px Mapterhorn tile at its zoom), as the protocol runs: the difference of the one-level and kLarge-level low-passes (lrm's half-pixel recentring, Float32 as the app keeps it), strict 3x3 extrema past the relief threshold, the strongest first, at most 2000, greedy suppression within half the tell size, then the blobness, plan convexity and Hessian vetoes on the low-pass ("coarse") or the tile's pixels ("fine"), pits on the flipped surface, and the half-maximum diameter on 8 rays. Holes (NaN) are the app's `paddedValid`. The vector form is a point layer for the view (the map's zoom, capped at z15 like the service's tiles), styled as the markers (`live/layers.py style_mounds`: the outline and pit colours, the measured diameter times the multiplier in ground metres).

Against the service's `moundDetector` vector tiles (`test_live.py` section 2c, `qgis-plugin/mvt_reader.py` decodes them): Mont Blanc z12 (1524 points), z13 (893), z14 (223), z13 with both polarities, radius 16 and size 60 (1668), z13 with the fine veto and a blobness threshold (635): **every point at the same position with the same measures (100 %)**, 0.3 to 1.3 s a tile in numpy.

### Any GDAL raster as the source

The panel's source list now holds the project's raster layers ("QGIS layer: ..."), and the box takes a file path, a `/vsicurl/` address, an https GeoTIFF or VRT, a `WCS:` string or a GDAL WMS description. `live/gdal_source.py`:

- **Probe**, once: the raster's extent in EPSG:3857 and lon/lat (its edge densified), the zoom of its resolution (one pixel's size in Web Mercator metres at the centre), its band's no data, scale and offset, its vertical unit (feet, US survey feet, from the band's unit type or a compound CRS's vertical axis).
- **Tiles**: one `gdal.Warp` per zoom to a VRT on that zoom's own pixel grid over the raster (the transformer and the overview made once; GDAL picks the overview for the zoom), its XML shared between threads; a 256 px tile is one window of it. No data becomes NaN, scale and offset are applied, feet become metres. A tile outside the raster is NaN without a read. Everything else is the tile pyramids' machinery (`live/dem.py`): the LRU of tiles, the padded grids from the neighbours, the ancestors for LRM and local dominance, the same kernels. A QGIS layer of another provider than GDAL's (QGIS's own WMS or WCS) is read through its provider and QGIS's raster projector instead.
- A full `gdal.Warp` per tile was the first try: 40 to 60 ms a tile, and threads did not overlap. The VRT window is 6 to 14 ms a tile in one thread, 2 to 5 ms in eight.

Measured (`test_live.py` section 7) on a Lambert-93 COG made from Mapterhorn around Mont Blanc (`make_test_dem.py`: 20 m, 1073 x 1070, three overviews, a 40-pixel hole of no data) and the same in feet without overviews:

| | |
|---|---|
| Probe | maxzoom 13 (28.7 m in Web Mercator), no data -9999, feet scale 0.3048 |
| Elevation against Mapterhorn (warped twice) | median 0.44 m at z12 and z13, 0.71 m at z14 |
| The feet file | within 0.0005 m of the metres one |
| The hole | NaN in the tile, the slope NaN around it only |
| 9 z14 tiles in parallel | about 0.6 s cold (each of 8 threads opens the file, PROJ and its VRT), 31 ms warm |
| One tile in one thread | 6 to 14 ms warm; the first per zoom 30 to 50 ms |
| A sky view factor tile on it | 36 ms (fast precision, elevation cached) |
| Mapterhorn, for comparison | 9 z13 tiles over the network about 1 s, cached 0.1 ms |

With a tile service on this machine, the same layer is registered with it (`POST /files`, [local files](/dev/tile-service#local-files)) and read there through the app's VRT reader: 3.2 m (median) from Mapterhorn on the same file, against GDAL's 0.4 m, an open question about that reader.

## Fourth round: the remaining modes, a registry the panel can grey out

After 0.6.0, the same day: every terrain mode of the app and the tile service has a Python port except the mound detector. The new code is in `live/kernels.py` (structure tensor, local dominance, box blur, line masks), `live/overlays.py` (graticule, app color ramps), `live/buildings.py` (building shadows) and `live/contours.py` (`view_values`, `view_vector`: the vector forms); `live/modes.py` registers them.

### What was added and how well it matches

| Mode | Port of | Against | Match |
|---|---|---|---|

Phong and matcap were already in 0.6.0 and still match the service (100 % within 3 levels, median 0). The terrain shadows (`kernels.shadow`) still match `lib/shadow-kernel.ts` with 0 pixels different.

**Orientation is not mirrored.** Aspect was (fixed 2026-11-25) because Horn's dx is west minus east and only it was negated. The structure tensor's gx and gy are both negated slopes and the tensor is quadratic in them, so its axis is the true one: a plane rising to the north-east reads 45°, to the north-west 135° (`lib/blobness-protocol.test.ts`, and section 0 of `test_live.py` on the Python port). It is the axis of the dominant **gradient**, across a ridge, not along it: a ridge running north-south reads 90°. The mode description used to say "which way a linear feature runs"; it now says what the code computes (no change to the values).

**Building shadows on roofs.** Off the footprints the two agree within a pixel. On them they differ: the service cuts the footprints out with one even-odd path over every building, so where two building parts overlap (a tower on its podium) the overlap is counted twice and the roof stays shaded (45 % of the footprint pixels in midtown Manhattan); here every roof is lit, as the app's description says.

### Registry metadata for the panel

Every entry of `MODES` now has `outputs` (of `colour`, `values`, `vector`; `forms_of` reads it), `python_supported` (False for the mound detector, listed so the panel can grey it out), `service` (its `/processes` id: elevation is `colorRelief`, the multidirectional hillshade `hillshade` with `service_query`), and where it applies `opacity` (the color layer's default), `stops` (the app's ramp from `lib/color-ramps.ts`, used for the color tiles of the new ramp modes, so they match the app), `vector_layer` (geometry and fields of the vector form). `ORDER` is the app's stacking order, top first; `PYTHON_MODES` the computed ones. `prefetch(mode, grid, opts)` fetches what a mode reads (the server calls it), `contours.view_vector(dem, extent, width_px, mode, opts)` returns any vector form as `[(wkb, attributes)]`.

## Third round: Color and Values per mode, Phong, matcap, contour lines, resampling

Plugin 0.6.0, the same day, after a first try in QGIS 4.2 by the user: checking only "values" next to curvature drew nothing, toggling did not add or remove layers reliably, and QGIS 4 showed no local compute at all. The last one was an old install: both QGIS profiles still had 0.2.2.

### A mode's row

Every mode row, of the tile service's groups and of the local compute box alike, is the same widget (`FormsRow` in `dock.py`): the mode's checkbox on the left, its forms on the right, **Color**, **Values** and, where there is one, **Vector**, then the settings cog.

- Checking the mode turns its Color layer on (the first time; afterwards the forms it had). Each form can be checked alone or with the others; checking a form checks the mode, unchecking the last one unchecks it.
- Each form is one layer, **added when checked and removed when unchecked**. 0.5.0 created the values layer only when the mode was also checked, and unchecking only hid layers, so a box and the layer tree could disagree.
- A layer removed by hand (or by **File > New**) unchecks its form: the panel listens to the project's `layersWillBeRemoved` and tells its own removals apart.
- The cog shows while Color (or Vector) is on, with a fixed place so that the boxes line up from row to row.
- In the local box, **Color** is the server's PNG tiles, now colored with the mode's ramp: QGIS's own ramp sampled at 256 steps on the main thread, so the same colors as the values layer's default renderer; grey and multiplied for the shades, RGB for matcap, the two browns of the mask on a clear background for contours. **Values** is the Float32 layer GDAL reads, as before. The **Draw as** choice is gone. A `c=2` parameter in the color tiles' address keeps QGIS's disk cache from serving 0.5.0's grey tiles, which had the same address.

Checked in QGIS 4.2 and 3.44: `test_plugin.py` (section 2d) and `test_live.py` (section 6) walk curvature through values only, both, mode off, mode on (both back), Color off, Values off, mode on (Color back), and a layer removed by hand, comparing the layers in the project and the cog after each step.

| Mode | Port of | Against | Match |

Phong's values are the multiply factor plus the highlight (0 to 1, highlights above). The color layer is their grey, multiplied over the layers under it: the same as the tile service's Phong layer in QGIS (its picture is the shading over white, also multiplied), so a highlight brighter than the layer under it is not drawn in either. Matcap is a picture, with no values form.

**Contour lines as a vector layer, not vector tiles.** For each view, once the canvas stops (400 ms), a `QgsTask` mosaics the elevation tiles over the view (the zoom whose pixel is the canvas pixel, coarser until 64 tiles or fewer cover it), runs `gdal.ContourGenerate` at the minor interval, and replaces the features of a memory layer (`elev`, `major`). A later view's result wins over an older one still running. QGIS redraws a memory layer at once on pan and zoom, the lines run whole across the view (no tile seams or clipping), and Identify, the attribute table and Save As work. A vector tile path would mean a tile server writing MVT, with lines cut at every tile edge. The trade-off: the layer holds the lines of the last view only (a frozen copy keeps them).

### Resampling and tile zoom

Measured headless in QGIS 4.2 and 3.44 (same results in both) with `QgsMapRendererParallelJob`, a 1024×768 canvas whose pixel is the pixel of 256 px tiles at zoom 12.0, 12.3, 12.5 and 12.7, the localhost server logging the zoom of each tile asked for (`LiveServer.seen`):

| Canvas zoom | XYZ (color) layers ask | GDAL (values) layers ask |
|---|---|---|
| 12.0 | z12, 20 tiles | z12, 20 tiles |
| 12.3 | z12, 16 tiles (scaled up 1.23×) | z13, 48 tiles (scaled down) |
| 12.5 | z13, 35 tiles | z13, 35 tiles |
| 12.7 | z13, 24 tiles | z13, 24 tiles |
| 12.0, device pixel ratio 2 | z13, 63 tiles | z13, 63 tiles |
| 12.3, device pixel ratio 2 | z13, 48 tiles | z14, 154 tiles |

- **The XYZ provider rounds** the canvas zoom to the nearest tile zoom; **GDAL takes the level just finer** (an overview is used only when at least as fine as the request). So between zooms x.0 and x.5, a values layer reads up to three times the tiles of the color layer: right for numbers (never scaled up), and only on that half of the range.
- **The device pixel ratio is honoured**: at a ratio of 2 both ask one zoom deeper (four times the tiles, sharp on a HiDPI screen). No double fetching, no tiles of the wrong zoom.
- **Default resampling** of every raster layer: nearest neighbour zoomed in and out, late (after the renderer), maximum oversampling 2.

What resampling changes, measured by drawing the contour mask (two colors) at zooms 12.3 and 12.7 and counting the colors:

| Layer, setting | Colors | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| XYZ, default | 5532 / 2759 | the provider already scales its tiles smoothly into the canvas |
| XYZ, bilinear (late) | 4881 / 2535 | a second resampling of the already scaled picture: slightly blurrier |
| XYZ, early resampling | 1 / 1 | nothing drawn: the XYZ provider cannot resample (`ProviderHintCanPerformProviderResampling` is false) |
| GDAL values, default | 3 / 3 | nearest: pixels dropped or doubled between zooms |
| GDAL values, bilinear late | 2362 / 2269 | the rendered colors blended |
| GDAL values, bilinear early | 3 / 3 for the mask's palette | GDAL resamples the values before the renderer: one resampling, of the numbers (a palette still shows only its classes) |

What the plugin does since 0.6.0:

- **Color (XYZ) layers: nothing.** The provider's own smooth scaling is the single resampling; a resampler would add a second one, and early resampling blanks the layer (worth knowing before ticking it in the layer's properties).
- **Values (GDAL) layers: early resampling, bilinear zoomed in and out**, set once when the layer is made (`values_resampling` in `layers.py`), for the tile service's values and the local ones alike; the contour mask (a palette) stays nearest. Identify reads the provider at the point, never the resampled picture.
- **A user's choice is kept.** A changed setting re-points the layer with `setDataSource`, which keeps the resampling, renderer, opacity and blending (checked in both versions). The local layers used to be removed and made again on every change, losing all of them; they now use `setDataSource` too, and the panel's opacity is applied to a new layer only.
- The local color tiles now go to z18 (Mapterhorn's 512 px z17), the depth the values layers already read.

`test_live.py` checks that a values layer gets early bilinear resampling and that a new setting keeps a layer's own opacity and resampler.

**A trap when comparing against a tile service**: every service on a machine keeps its tiles in the same disk cache by default (`%TEMP%/terrain-viewer-tiles`, keyed by address), so a service of a newer commit serves the tiles an older one wrote. Aspect scored 0.4 % against a same-commit service until it was started with its own `TILE_CACHE_DIR`: the tiles were from before the aspect fix. With its own cache, every mode scores 100 % in QGIS 4.2 and 3.44, Phong and matcap included (median difference 0 grey levels).

### Values and color ramps: a GDAL source, not a Python provider

The first round's open question was how to give QGIS **values** (so that QGIS's own color ramps, Identify, the raster calculator and Processing work) rather than pictures colored in numpy. Two designs were on the table:

| | Registered Python raster provider | The localhost server's Float32 tiles read by GDAL (built) |
|---|---|---|
| How | a `QgsRasterDataProvider` subclass; QGIS calls `block(band, extent, w, h)` for the whole canvas | `/{mode}/{z}/{x}/{y}.tif` (Float32, NaN no data) and a GDAL WMS description (TMS minidriver, the same as the tile service's `/gdal.xml`), opened by QGIS's own `gdal` provider; the XML is the layer's source |
| Who computes | QGIS's render thread, holding the GIL through the Python parts; one block for the whole canvas, nothing shown until it is done | the server's threads; GDAL asks up to 16 tiles at once (`MaxConnections`) and caches the blocks it read |
| Values in QGIS | yes | yes: Identify, raster calculator, zonal statistics, Processing, Save As GeoTIFF |
| Styling | QGIS's renderers | QGIS's renderers (the plugin sets a colored default: a ramp of QGIS's style over the mode's range, grey and multiplied for the shades, two colors for the contour mask) |
| Outside QGIS | no | yes: the same XML opens in gdalinfo, gdal_translate, rasterio |
| Saved project | needs the provider registered before the project opens | needs the server running (it asks port 3420 first, so a reopened project's layers work again once the box is checked) |
| Risk | the traps of the first probe (exit 127 on a wrong capabilities type, objects collected under QGIS) | GDAL WMS is a mature driver; the server is the one already built |

**Chosen: GDAL over the server's Float32 tiles.** It reuses the server, keeps the computing off QGIS's render thread, is parallel by construction, works outside QGIS, and has the same shape as the tile service's values, so the panel treats both alike. A Terrarium-encoded PNG read through a VRT was ruled out: it quantises (1/256 m) and needs a pixel function to decode. The grey image tiles stay as **Draw as: Grey image tiles (XYZ)**, the form QGIS shows progressively, tile by tile.

The colored default per mode: hypsometric ramp (the plugin's own: QGIS's style has none) over the 2nd to 98th percentile of the elevation in view, YlOrRd for slope, Turbo for aspect, RdBu for curvature, TPI and LRM, Magma for TRI and roughness, reversed Greys for sky view factor and openness. Any of them can be changed in the layer's **Symbology** like any raster's.

### Parallelism

The first server already gave each request a thread (`ThreadingHTTPServer`) and fetched a tile's neighbours in parallel. This round added:

- **A compute pool sized to the cores**: a semaphore with one slot per logical core around the numpy part; fetching happens before taking a slot, so a slow network never holds a core.
- **The listen backlog**: socketserver's default queue of 5 connections overflowed as soon as more than 5 clients connected at once, and Windows retries a refused connection after about half a second. Sixteen clients got 60 to 75 tiles/s from the first server whatever the mode; raised to 128, 300 to 650.
- **Caches**: decoded elevation within a 256 MB budget (it was 512 tiles of up to 1 MB each), and the finished tiles (64 MB LRU), so a re-render or the same mode as values and as a picture is not computed twice.
- **Prefetch**: when the canvas moves, its elevation tiles and a ring around are fetched in the background before QGIS asks.
- **A fix**: the address of a grey XYZ layer was written into QGIS's source by hand, so its settings were cut at the first `&` (a mode with two settings lost the second); it is now encoded by `QgsDataSourceUri`, which each QGIS version reads back (a percent-encoded address written by hand is decoded by 4.2, not by 3.44).

Measured with `qgis-plugin/bench_canvas.py`: a 1920×1080 canvas at z12, z13 and z14 around Mont Blanc (48, 40 and 45 tiles), elevation already cached, every tile fresh (a cache-busting parameter), median of 3 runs, QGIS 4.2 on the 20-core laptop; "before" is the cb7c545 server, timed the same way.

| Server, form | Hillshade z12 / z13 / z14 | Sky view factor r = 16 | Shadows, sun 30° |
|---|---|---|---|
| before, grey XYZ | 33 / 34 / 40 tiles/s (1.1 to 1.5 s a canvas) | 35 / 35 / 35 | 36 / 39 / 37 |
| after, grey XYZ | 54 / 57 / 54 (0.7 to 0.9 s) | 43 / 50 / 46 | 53 / 54 / 57 |
| after, values (GDAL) | 244 / 206 / 100 (0.2 to 0.45 s) | 104 / 83 / 93 (0.46 to 0.48 s) | 149 / 118 / 147 (0.3 to 0.34 s) |

QGIS 3.44, the same runs: before, grey XYZ 31 to 56 tiles/s (0.84 to 1.5 s a canvas); after, grey XYZ 28 to 46 (no gain: QGIS's pace, within the noise); after, values: hillshade 134 to 143 (0.3 s), sky view factor 57 to 62 (0.65 to 0.84 s), shadows 91 to 108 (0.42 to 0.48 s).

- **The XYZ form is limited by QGIS**, not by the server: QGIS's XYZ provider has at most 6 tiles in flight (the server saw a peak of 6) and paces them at about 50 tiles/s. GDAL asks 16 at a time, so the values form, the default, is 2.5 to 7 times faster than before (1.5 to 3 times in 3.44).

- The server alone, the 40 z13 tiles from 16 client threads: hillshade 278, shadows 110 to 180, sky view factor 60 to 90 tiles/s (before: 60 to 75 for every mode, the backlog).
- Laptop noise is large (±30 % between runs); the ranking holds across runs.

## First round

### What was built

- **Elevation** (`live/dem.py`): Mapterhorn's 512 px WebP Terrarium tiles (or the AWS Terrarium PNGs), decoded by `QImage`. Both QGIS 4.2 (Qt 6.11) and 3.44 (Qt 5.15) list `webp` among their image formats, and `QImage` is reentrant, so it decodes in worker threads. The RGB packing is turned into metres with numpy. The plugin always serves 256 px tiles: with a 512 px source, tile z/x/y is one quarter of tile z-1. Past the source's depth (a 404), the parent tile is upsampled. The padded grid is the 3×3 tiles around the one asked for (fetched in parallel), cropped to the halo, so the halo is at most 256 px. Decoded tiles go into an LRU cache with in-flight de-duplication, because the nine neighbours of one tile are shared by nine requests.
- **Kernels** (`live/kernels.py`), vectorised numpy written against an array module (`xp`), so cupy runs the same code:
  - **Hillshade / slope**: Horn's 3×3 gradient, halo 1.
  - **Sky view factor**: `lib/horizon-kernel.ts` "precise". For each of 8 directions, every pixel of the radius as one shifted-array operation over the whole tile; `sin(atan t)` is computed as `t / sqrt(1 + t²)`, and steps that land on the same pixel are skipped.
  - **Shadows**: `computeShadowTile` of `lib/shadow-kernel.ts`, the shadow-height sweep, with the same reach and the same exact early exit. The JavaScript walks the sheared grid row by row. Here the whole sheared grid is one gather, and the van Herk / Gil-Werman prefix and suffix maxima are two `maximum.accumulate` calls over blocks of J rows.
  - `live/kernels_numba.py` holds the same kernels as loops for numba (`prange` over rows; over sheared columns for the sweep). It is only used when numba imports, and QGIS ships without numba.
- **Delivery** (`live/server.py`): a `ThreadingHTTPServer` on `127.0.0.1` on a free port, on a daemon thread, answering `/{mode}/{z}/{x}/{y}.png?<settings>` with a greyscale PNG (zlib level 1) and `Cache-Control: max-age=86400`. Standard QGIS XYZ layers read from it (`live/layers.py`).
- **Checked against the app** (`qgis-plugin/test_live.py`): `live_reference.mjs` runs the app's own TypeScript kernels in Node on the same padded grids. On Mont Blanc's z12 tile, under six suns (azimuths 0 to 315°, altitudes 5 to 45°), the numpy shadows differ from `computeShadowTile` by **0 pixels**. The sky view factor at radius 8 and 32 differs by at most 0.004 %, which is the Terrarium quantisation of the reference's output. numba gives the same results as numpy. The same script then draws a 1024×768 canvas of each mode with `QgsMapRendererParallelJob` and checks the panel's checkbox (the group appears when it is checked and goes away, with the server, when it is unchecked). It passes in QGIS 4.2 and 3.44.

#### What the numbers say

- **numpy is enough for these three modes.** Every mode stays under 15 ms per tile in one thread, below the cost of a network fetch of the tile. Shadows and hillshade are memory-bound array operations, and the vectorised sweep is as fast as V8 running the app's own loop.
- **Threads help when the operations are large.** numpy releases the GIL inside each array operation. The shadow sweep is a few large gathers and scans, and gains 2 to 3× from 8 threads. The sky view factor is hundreds of small operations on 64k pixels (8 directions × radius), so threads mostly wait on the GIL and gain nothing (slightly worse at r = 8).
- **numba**, when it is there, is the cheapest speed-up: 1.5 to 2× on the sky view factor over all cores, and a 0.1 ms hillshade. But QGIS ships without it, and pip-installing into QGIS's Python is not something a plugin should do. The plugin would use it only when it imports (`kernels_numba.available`).
- **cupy is slower than numpy per tile.** A 256 px tile is too little work for a GPU. Each array operation is one kernel launch (dozens per tile for the sky view factor), plus the transfers. A fused `RawKernel` with one thread per pixel would remove the launches, but the transfers stay. The GPU pays off on a whole canvas computed in one dispatch, like the app's live GPU shadows over a Float32 view mosaic, not on tiles.
- **Against the tile service**, the local tiles were 5 to 100× faster, end to end. The service pays for its whole pipeline on each tile: the layer stack rebuilt from URL parameters, a worker hop, the app's color paint and PNG encoding by sharp. Its sky view factor (1 to 2 s per tile, even at r = 8) was far above what the kernel costs in Node (60 to 110 ms in the column above): each 256 px tile computed four 512 px source tiles. Fixed on 2026-11-25 (see the note under the table): the service now comes within 1.5 to 3× of the local server, slower mostly on tiles whose 512 px source tile is new.

**Chosen for the prototype: (c).** It is the least code, uses only API that QGIS 3.28 already has (tested in 3.44 and 4.2), and leaves tiling, threading, caching, resampling between zoom levels and reprojection to QGIS. Option (d) is the right shape for exports and batch runs. For values, the second round kept (c) and added Float32 tiles that GDAL reads (see [values and color ramps](#values-and-color-ramps-a-gdal-source-not-a-python-provider)) rather than option (a), whose probe worked in both versions but found two traps the hard way:

- `capabilities()` must return the flag type the version expects (`Qgis.RasterInterfaceCapability` in 3.44 and 4.x). Returning the old `QgsRasterDataProvider.Size | Identify` makes 3.44 exit with no traceback (exit code 127).
- The Python objects returned by the factory and by `clone()` must be kept referenced, or they are collected while QGIS still uses them.

## GPU "at shader level"

- **QGIS's own raster shading** is a fixed C++ pipeline (`QgsRasterRenderer` subclasses: single band, pseudocolor, hillshade). A Python `QgsRasterRenderer` subclass is possible in principle, but it is called with blocks in a render thread like a provider. It is not a shader hook, and it can only color values a provider already computed. The 2D canvas has no GLSL entry point; QGIS 3D's materials do, but they do not apply to 2D maps.
- **OpenGL compute through Qt**: PyOpenGL ships with QGIS on Windows (3.1.10). A `QOffscreenSurface` and `QOpenGLContext` on a worker thread could run the app's shaders. Per tile, it would pay the same upload and readback costs as cupy. It only makes sense for a canvas-sized mosaic in one dispatch, the way the app's live shadows work.
- **cupy**: measured above. It works when installed (it needs the CUDA wheels), and it is slower than numpy per tile.
- **Verdict**: CPU numpy for tiles now. A GPU path only as a canvas-mosaic renderer (option a or b computing the whole view at once), and only if a mode is too slow on the CPU, which none of these three is.

## Limits of the prototype

- Sixteen modes; the whole-view modes stay on the tile service (above).
- Elevation from Mapterhorn (or AWS) only (since: any pyramid of the library, any GDAL raster). The halo is at most one tile (256 px), which is enough for the sweep's default radius but not for its 512 px cap.
- A project saved with the local layers points at the server's port: it asks 3420 first, so the layers work again once the box is checked after reopening (not when that port was taken and another was given).
- Elevation is fetched with `urllib`, which ignores QGIS's proxy and authentication settings. `QgsBlockingNetworkRequest` would respect them (the tile service installer uses it).
- The sky view factor and openness were the "precise" variant; "fast", the app's default, is ported since the fifth round.

## Related work and references

Tools that already compute terrain modes inside QGIS, and how they relate to this experiment (links checked 2026-11-25). They all work the same way: an algorithm reads a raster layer and writes a new raster, for the extent and resolution chosen when it runs. This experiment instead computes tiles as the canvas asks for them, from a tile pyramid. The papers behind each mode are on [Terrain Analysis Pipeline, related work](/dev/terrain-analysis-pipeline#related-work-and-references).

- **[gdaldem](https://gdal.org/en/stable/programs/gdaldem.html)**, in the Processing toolbox under GDAL > Raster analysis: hillshade (also `-multidirectional`, `-combined`, `-igor`), slope, aspect, TRI, TPI and roughness. The reference the table above checks against.
- **[Relief Visualization Toolbox](https://plugins.qgis.org/plugins/rvt-qgis/)** (QGIS plugin of [RVT_py](https://github.com/EarthObservation/RVT_py)): hillshade from one or several directions, slope, simple local relief model, sky-view factor (also anisotropic), positive and negative openness, local dominance, multi-scale topographic position and the VAT blends. It covers the relief modes ported here and several that are not (local dominance, the blends). Its sky-view factor uses the same per-direction formula over 16 directions by default, against 8 here and in the app.
- **[WhiteboxTools](https://github.com/jblindsay/whitebox-tools)** ([geomorphometric tools](https://jblindsay.github.io/wbt_book/available_tools/geomorphometric_analysis.html); in QGIS through Alexander Bruy's [Whitebox for Processing](https://jblindsay.github.io/wbt_book/qgis_plugin.html) plugin, which needs the WhiteboxTools program installed): slope, aspect, profile, plan and other curvatures, RuggednessIndex, DiffFromMeanElev and DevFromMeanElev, MaxElevationDeviation and MultiscaleTopographicPositionImage, MultidirectionalHillshade, HorizonAngle. Its [RuggednessIndex](https://jblindsay.github.io/wbt_book/available_tools/geomorphometric_analysis.html#ruggednessindex) divides by the number of neighbours (a root-mean-square), where the app and gdaldem keep Riley's undivided root, so its values are those here divided by √8. [DiffFromMeanElev](https://jblindsay.github.io/wbt_book/available_tools/geomorphometric_analysis.html#difffrommeanelev) is the app's TPI over a window of any size, computed with an integral image so the cost does not grow with the window.
- **SAGA GIS** (Conrad et al. 2015, below; in QGIS through the [Processing Saga NextGen Provider](https://plugins.qgis.org/plugins/processing_saga_nextgen/) plugin, with SAGA installed separately): slope, aspect and curvatures by several methods, Zevenbergen and Thorne's 9-parameter polynomial among them (the fit the app's profile and plan curvatures use), TPI, TRI, sky-view factor and topographic openness.
- **GRASS GIS** (QGIS's GRASS Processing provider): [r.relief](https://grass.osgeo.org/grass-stable/manuals/r.relief.html) (hillshade from one azimuth and altitude), [r.slope.aspect](https://grass.osgeo.org/grass-stable/manuals/r.slope.aspect.html) (slope, aspect, profile and tangential curvature, and the partial derivatives), [r.param.scale](https://grass.osgeo.org/grass-stable/manuals/r.param.scale.html) (slope, curvatures and landform classes from a quadratic fitted by least squares to a window of any size, where the app's 3×3 modes have one fixed size). The addons [r.skyview](https://grass.osgeo.org/grass-stable/manuals/addons/r.skyview.html) (sky-view factor from `r.horizon`, or positive openness with `-o`) and [r.local.relief](https://grass.osgeo.org/grass-stable/manuals/addons/r.local.relief.html) (Hesse's full local relief model, which the app simplifies, see [LRM](/dev/lrm#related-work-and-references)) are installed in GRASS, not with QGIS.
