about=A dock panel with the groups of Terrain Viewer's sidebar, one modes tree whatever draws it. Pick a backend at the top (radio buttons): Python backend (the modes computed in QGIS itself with numpy, no service, started when picked: every mode, from tile pyramids, COGs, VRTs, raw WMS and WCS, QGIS raster layers and anything GDAL opens), Packaged tile server (the Terrain Viewer tile service packaged for QGIS, Node and the service, about 50 MB, downloaded from the project's GitHub release the first time, its sha256 checked, started on this machine when you connect, stopped with QGIS), or Remote tile server (an address: the Terrain Viewer desktop app's, http://localhost:3400, one you run, or a hosted one). Each checked mode becomes a layer, with its settings in the layer's address; changing a setting rewrites the address. Each row has its opacity and its forms: Color (or Raster), Values (a Float32 raster for Identify, the raster calculator, Processing) and, for the overlays, Vector (vector tiles, the default). Sliders with number boxes, ramp and matcap selects with previews, a light direction pad shared by the shaded modes (free, or the sun at a date and time over the map's centre), contour labels, symmetric ramps, the source picker in groups (the project's elevation layers, the worldwide defaults, the terrain library) with a zoom to the source and the raw elevation of a library COG, VRT or WMS added as a QGIS layer, a basemap row (the app's built-in basemaps), API keys behind a key. "Open link" loads the layers of a share link (or its query). "Freeze" copies the layers drawn now into a group the panel never changes, to compare settings. Updates: add the plugin repository https://github.com/Iconem/terrain-viewer/releases/download/qgis-plugins-repository/plugins.xml in the plugin manager's settings, or press Update in the panel when it says a newer version is out. The panel is built from what the service answers, so new modes and settings appear without a plugin update. A Historical mode (the clock at the top, or the clock button of the Web toolbar; the app's historical imagery mode) lists the capture dates of Esri World Imagery Wayback or Google Earth historical (through a tile server backend) at the map's centre, or Planet's monthly and quarterly basemaps (with a Planet key), NASA HLS's monthly mosaics or EOX's yearly Sentinel-2 cloudless mosaics, and adds one layer per date with its temporal range: QGIS's Temporal Controller steps through the dates (a regular step, one week by default, a day, a month or a year, proportional to time, each frame drawing the latest capture at or before it; or one stop per capture date), plays and exports them; the backend control shows on that page while a source needs a tile server. Building shadows take their buildings from OpenStreetMap (OpenFreeMap), Overture, another vector source with heights (a PMTiles archive, a TileJSON, a vector tile template) or a polygon layer of the project with its height field. Pure Python, no dependency.

changelog=0.11.0
    * Temporal Controller: the default step is one week (0.10.1: source timestamps), so the slider's position is the date; a day, a month, a year and source timestamps stay selectable. Every dated layer stands from its true capture date until the next capture's, so each frame draws the latest capture at or before it, and under the dates the panel names both: "Controller: week of 2019-06-03. Imagery: captured 2018-09-11, the latest at or before it."
    * Historical page: the backend control (Python backend, Packaged tile server, Remote tile server, with Connect and the status line) shows there while the source picked needs a tile server (Google Earth historical), the very control of the Terrain page, one setting and one connection; Google Earth historical can be picked before a tile server is connected. The sources that need no backend (Esri Wayback, Planet, NASA HLS, EOX) say so
    * Building shadows: "Buildings from", under the row's cog, with every backend: a vector source of the app's Library that has heights (OpenStreetMap buildings from OpenFreeMap, the default; Overture Maps buildings; ...), an address of your own (a PMTiles archive, a TileJSON or a {z}/{x}/{y} vector tile template, with its layer and height field), or a polygon layer of the project with its height field (metres per unit, a height for the buildings without one, or one height for all). The choice is kept in the project
    * A polygon layer is written to a GeoJSON in the profile: the Python backend burns it, a tile service on this machine reads it once registered (a tile service that answers /building-sources, as the bundles of this release; an older one draws OpenFreeMap's buildings, said once). A layer over 50 000 features is read for the map's extent, again when the map leaves it
    * Python backend: PMTiles archives read in plain Python (range requests, the header and directories once), Overture's current release found from its catalog
    * QGIS 3.44: a script or a session that drew building shadows with the Python backend no longer crashes Python as QGIS quits (an OGR dataset outlived GDAL)

    * Esri Wayback: the dates are added to the list, the axis and the layer group as they answer, also after a pan (the layers on screen stay meanwhile); a notice says when the dates shown are those of the previous place, and when the view is too wide for capture dates, with the zoom needed; the releases that can hold the date asked for are dated first; the walk and the dates found are kept between sessions (a place seen before costs one request)
    * Google Earth historical, through a tile server backend (its dates at the place and plain Web Mercator tiles of a date: a tile service from 2026-11-26 or later); greyed out with the reason without one
    * Planet monthly and quarterly basemaps, with a Planet key under a folded "API keys" on the Historical page (kept in QGIS's settings, in the address of the Planet layers only, shown in no message); NASA HLS's monthly mosaics

    0.10.0
    * Historical mode, a first version of the web app's historical imagery mode: a switch at the top of the panel (Terrain, Historical with a clock). Its panel: the imagery (Esri World Imagery Wayback, EOX Sentinel-2 cloudless, or one of the worldwide default basemaps), the dates available at the map's centre as a list and as ticks on a time axis, previous and next, the current date and who took it. With every backend and without one: the tiles come from the providers
    * The dates are steps of QGIS's own Temporal Controller: each date is one XYZ layer of the group "Historical imagery" with a fixed temporal range (its capture until the next one, a yearly mosaic's year), and the controller is set to animated navigation with the step "source timestamps", so its slider has one stop per real capture date and stepping it swaps the imagery; picking a date in the panel moves it, and the other way round. With the controller off, the date's layer alone is checked
    * Esri Wayback's dates are the real capture dates at the place (the releases with their own imagery there, then each one's metadata), listed in the background as they answer; a pan to another tile lists them again (Follow the map); zoomed out past zoom 10, the newest release alone
    * Open link reads a link in the app's historical mode (appMode=historical, historicalActiveSourceA, dateA)
    * Not yet: Google Earth historical, Planet, NASA HLS, the national series and the catalogs
    0.9.3

    0.9.2
